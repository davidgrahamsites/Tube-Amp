Preamp supply load and regulator boundary

The passive supply cannot enforce the proposed 300 V ceiling during tube warm-up. A regulated candidate retains approximately 25 W in the generic audio model, but its 3.3 kΩ input resistor causes dropout at the low diagnostic input. Do not adopt this resistor or release dependent routing.

What changed in the test

HD-003 held the early preamp supply at 278 V to isolate the heater-reference proposal. This test restores R17, the 10 kΩ connection from B_PRE to B_EARLY, and its 50 µF filter. C13 and C25 each add 50 µF at B_PRE. The proposed heater reference draws through 39 kΩ + 39 kΩ. Native circuit, schematic and PCB files remain unchanged.

The first HD-004 run varied B_SCREEN, which also supplies the output-tube screens; its 34.90 W result at 550 V was therefore unsuitable for an isolated preamp comparison. The corrected fixture varies a separate SUPPLY_IN source and holds the output screens at 380 V. The rejected receipt preserves the error and original metrics; its hashes do not describe the replacement decks. The independent sources are a diagnostic fixture, not a proposed complete power supply.

Calculations

Cold tubes are represented by removing their model instances; this gives a static no-tube-current check, not a heater warm-up model. The two reference resistors remain connected.

\[V_{\mathrm{pre,cold}}=V_{\mathrm{in}}\frac{R_{\mathrm{ref}}}{R_{35}+R_{\mathrm{ref}}}=380\frac{78000}{10000+78000}=336.818\ \mathrm{V}.\]

R17 supplies the early stages. At the fixed 300 V test point:

\[I_{\mathrm{early}}=\frac{V_{\mathrm{pre}}-V_{\mathrm{early}}}{R_{17}}=\frac{300-266.478566}{10000}=3.35214\ \mathrm{mA}.\]

The candidate uses a 200 Ω upper programming resistor and a 43.2 kΩ lower resistor. These are electrical targets, not selected purchase items. With a 1.25 V reference and 50 µA adjustment current:

\[V_o=V_{\mathrm{ref}}\left(1+\frac{R_b}{R_t}\right)+I_{\mathrm{adj}}R_b=1.25(1+43200/200)+50\times10^{-6}(43200)=273.41\ \mathrm{V}.\]

Current balance at the adjustment pin gives the total programming load, including adjustment current:

\[I_{\mathrm{program,in}}=\frac{V_o+I_{\mathrm{adj}}R_t}{R_t+R_b}=\frac{273.41+50\times10^{-6}(200)}{43400}=6.300\ \mathrm{mA}.\]

Sixteen reference/current/resistance endpoints use 1.20–1.30 V, 0–100 µA and ±0.5% total resistance allowance. They produce 257.821–289.264 V; adding the allocated +1% drift gives 292.156 V. The +1% allowance is not a lifetime guarantee. Minimum programming output current is 5.970 mA; maximum lower-resistor dissipation is 1.910 W before the additional drift allowance. This resistor needs a qualified series network or suitable rated part.

The behavioral model reserves 10 V plus a current-dependent term across the pass assembly. At the nominal warm target, the maximum feed resistance before dropout at 320 V is approximately:

\[R_{\mathrm{feed,max}}=\frac{320-273.41-10}{0.01738442}-100\approx2005\ \Omega.\]

This nominal result rejects 3.3 kΩ; it does not qualify 2 kΩ. Repeat the budget with the highest target voltage, highest load, input ripple, resistor tolerance and real pass-device limits. Reducing the feed resistance transfers heat and fault stress into the pass assembly.

At the 550 V warm DC boundary, the behavioral pass assembly dissipates 3.811 W and the feed resistor dissipates 0.997 W. These values allocate normal-load heat only; they do not establish a transistor safe operating area or a short-circuit rating.

Part boundaries

TI LM317L-N, SNVS775L, pages 1 and 4–5: the floating regulator has a 40 V maximum input-output differential; it cannot directly absorb the tested supply drop. The 1.20–1.30 V reference range requires the stated current, differential and dissipation conditions. The full-range minimum-load requirement is 5 mA. The listed 1% long-term figure applies to 1,000 hours at 125°C. Candidate LM317LZ/NOPB belongs to this family; similarly named newer parts need their own review.

Microchip LR8, DS20005399B, pages 3–4: the specified input-output range ends at 450 V; current-limit minimum is 10 mA under the stated 12 V differential and temperature condition. The preamp/reference load already reaches 12.134 mA at 300 V. A direct LR8 replacement therefore lacks a demonstrated current guarantee, and a 550 V cold-start boundary needs separate protection. This rejects the direct substitution, not every possible LR8 circuit.

Evidence limits and next action

The limiter is an algebraic current source with a provisional 30 mA ceiling. It includes no transistor parasitics, regulator control loop, shutdown path or fault protection. Its programming-load equivalent follows DC current balance and is not a startup model. Tube and transformer models remain generic; the effects buffer is ideal. The 550 V diagnostic is not a selected transformer specification.

Next: select the actual input envelope and compare the smallest workable ceiling circuit against the floating-regulator option. Qualify pass-device DC safe operating area, differential protection, current limit, reverse discharge, temperature and startup before native adoption. Keep these parts inside the power functional block; export only the specified supply and return connections to the audio block. Chassis fit, complete routing and objective 6 remain open.

Vocabulary key

Dropout
Loss of voltage regulation because too little input voltage remains after circuit losses.
Screen
An electrode inside the output tube; its supply affects tube current and output power.
Programming network
Resistors that set a regulator's output voltage.
Pass assembly
Devices that control supply current and dissipate the excess voltage as heat.
Safe operating area
Allowed device current and voltage combinations for a stated duration and temperature.
Fundamental power
Power in the intended test frequency, excluding harmonics.
Harmonic distortion
Unwanted output at multiples of the test frequency; this report includes harmonics 2–10.
