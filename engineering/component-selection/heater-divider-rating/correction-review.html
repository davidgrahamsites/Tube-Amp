Split the heater references; bound the preamp supply

Conditional correction candidate. Separate the four preamp heaters from the two output-tube heaters. The calculations support further implementation work at a 300 V maximum preamp supply. The actual supply ceiling, fault protection and isolated windings are not implemented.

Problem and proposed circuit

The present common reference fails the earlier HD-002 screen. The output tubes need a low-resistance reference near ground; the dirty-channel cathode follower needs an elevated reference. Both halves of V3 share one heater, so moving only the output heaters does not resolve every high-supply case.

Primary limits

The JJ reference sheets specify 180 V heater-to-cathode and 150 kΩ heater-to-cathode resistance for ECC83S, and 100 V and 20 kΩ for EL34. The ECC803S sheet specifies 100 V; it is not a higher-limit substitute. Manufacturer documents were checked on 26 November 2026. Purchased tube identity remains open.

Inputs and calculation

Allocate ±5% total resistor variation, ±10% total capacitor variation, ±2% midpoint-resistor variation and 10 µA total reference leakage. These are design allocations, not selected-part guarantees. Use 8 V RMS as a heater excursion for this diagnostic only; it is not an approved operating voltage.

Let \(R_t,R_b\) be the upper and lower resistors, \(C_t,C_b\) their parallel capacitors, and \(u\) the B_PRE voltage. The reference transfer is:

\[H(s)=\frac{1/R_t+sC_t}{1/R_t+1/R_b+s(C_t+C_b)}=a_\infty+\frac{a_0-a_\infty}{1+s\tau}\]
\[a_0=\frac{R_b}{R_t+R_b},\qquad a_\infty=\frac{C_t}{C_t+C_b},\qquad \tau=(R_t\parallel R_b)(C_t+C_b)\]

The allocated ranges are \(0.475\le a_0\le0.525\) and \(0.45\le a_\infty\le0.55\). Nominal time constant is 3.9 ms. Equal ratios make the reference track half the supply; unequal ratios can produce a negative reference transient when the supply falls.

For a supply between zero and 300 V, a corresponding bounded filter state and a cathode between ground and the instantaneous supply, the worst heater-to-cathode allocation is:

\[V_{h,pk}=8\sqrt2\frac{1.02}{2}=5.76999\ \mathrm V\]
\[R_{th,max}=(39\,000\times1.05)/2=20\,475\ \Omega\]
\[|V_{hk}|\le0.55(300)+5.76999+10^{-5}(20\,475+102)=170.97576\ \mathrm V\]
\[M=180-170.97576=9.02424\ \mathrm V\]

The 102 Ω allowance uses one midpoint resistor, avoiding a dependency on unknown winding resistance. This voltage proof does not cover a cathode held above a collapsing supply by stored charge, a supply excursion above 300 V, excessive leakage or a component fault.

Include the cathode return resistance

The first 100 kΩ/100 kΩ candidate was rejected: its reference alone appeared acceptable, but the cold cathode-follower path also contains R22. At +5% variation the combined path exceeds 150 kΩ.

\[52\,500+102+105\,000=157\,602\ \Omega>150\,000\ \Omega\]

The revised 39 kΩ divider has these conservative cold paths; no conducting-tube impedance is credited:

\[R_{hk,max}=20\,475+102+105\,000=125\,577\ \Omega\]
\[R_{hk,source\ open}=40\,950+102+105\,000=146\,052\ \Omega\]

The second expression uses only the lower divider resistor when the upper supply path is open. The checker verifies each cathode resistor value and path against the source circuit. Cathode-resistor faults remain separate checks.

Power and loading

\[I_{divider}=\frac{300}{78\,000}=3.84615\ \mathrm{mA},\qquad P_R=I^2(39\,000)=0.576923\ \mathrm W\]

The resistor corner screen reaches 0.60729 W per divider resistor. A nominal 2 W class is a starting point; working voltage, hot derating, pulse rating, lead temperature and China availability still govern selection. The two 100 nF capacitors add a nominal 50 nF series load to B_PRE. Neither capacitor is the former 47 µF part.

Completed comparisons

Sixteen startup/shutdown simulations agree with the independent linear-ramp solution to less than 0.001 V. Eight single-component fault envelopes can reach about 305.77 V heater-to-cathode; none is accepted. Those are conservative envelopes, not simulated tube-fault waveforms.

Sampled small-signal gain changes stay below 0.08 dB at approximately 84 Hz, 1.02 kHz, 4.98 kHz, 10.22 kHz and 20 kHz. The 20 kHz changes are −0.0369 dB clean and −0.0448 dB dirty. This shows little incremental treble loss from the rail change; it does not clear the existing transformer-response or stability work.

Next engineering action

  3. Specify two isolated heater groups and check winding insulation, current, noise and thermal margins.
  4. After those checks, revise the readable schematic, parts and connector interfaces; then update placement and routing.
