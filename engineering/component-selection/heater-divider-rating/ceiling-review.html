Preamp ceiling and feed resistor

The proposed 680 Ω feed clears the previous voltage-sag problem within the declared model bounds. The lowest remaining voltage margin is 1.082 V. The real pass circuit, fault disconnection and temperature limits remain open; do not copy this behavioral regulator into a fabrication release.

Task and evidence

Owner: heater-divider-rating. Baseline: Git 77a1758. Inputs: the HD-005 contract, the unchanged HD-004 coupled-load adapter and the source/native hashes in that contract. Outputs: 36 DC and four audio results, 374 passing evidence checks and this report. The checks verify the stated model and saved waveforms; they do not qualify a physical regulator.

The 320, 380 and 550 V input values are diagnostic bounds, not guaranteed transformer outputs. Output-tube screen voltage stays at 380 V in every case. “Cold” removes tube instances for a static unpowered-tube load check; it does not model heater warm-up. See the HD-004 diagnosis for the rejected shared-screen experiment.

Why the existing shunt feed was rejected

A shunt clamp diverts current to hold the rail voltage. At 550 V input, any clamp holding the rail at or below 300 V dissipates at least the following power in the existing 10 kΩ feed, even at its +5% resistance corner:

\[P_{\mathrm{feed,min}}=\frac{(550-300)^2}{10000(1.05)}=5.952\ \mathrm{W}>5\ \mathrm{W}.\]

This rejects retaining the existing 5 W feed for that diagnostic condition. A larger resistor and a separately qualified clamp could still work, but would require a new heat and fault design. No zener circuit was adopted.

Exact feed candidate and resistance allowance

Candidate: Vishay PR03000206800JAC00, 680 Ω, ±5%, copper leads. The manufacturer's 8 July 2025 data specifies 3 W at 70 °C, 750 V working limit and ±250 ppm/K temperature coefficient for this variant. The rating still depends on the assembly's heat flow and film temperature.

The analysis allocates a maximum film temperature of 150 °C relative to 25 °C, then applies the published endurance, damp-heat and soldering shifts successively. Combining separate qualification-test shifts is a conservative engineering scenario; it is not a manufacturer lifetime guarantee.

\[t_T=250\times10^{-6}(150-25)=0.03125.\]

\[R_{0,-}=680(1-0.05)(1-t_T),\qquad R_{0,+}=680(1+0.05)(1+t_T).\]

For each stage use \(R_{n,-}=R_{n-1,-}(1-f_n)-a_n\) and \(R_{n,+}=R_{n-1,+}(1+f_n)+a_n\).

Voltage and heat results

All 36 declared static corners remain below 300 V and within 10 mV of their respective target. The highest calculated rail is 292.155 V. The most demanding low-input case draws 18.523 mA at a 292.154 V rail with an 805 Ω feed.

\[V_{\mathrm{pass}}=320-(0.0185230)(805)-292.1545=12.9345\ \mathrm{V}.\]

The behavioral regulator requires \(10+100I\) volts at current \(I\) in amperes, leaving:

\[M=12.9345-[10+100(0.0185230)]=1.0822\ \mathrm{V}.\]

This is a narrow, conditional margin. A real pass device, protection network or lower transformer voltage can consume it. Maximum feed heat across the 36 cases is 0.2762 W; maximum pass-assembly heat is 4.6495 W. These are separate maxima and exclude transient energy.

Faults still prevent adoption

With a working 30 mA current limit and a shorted output, the pass assembly could dissipate 15.987 W at 550 V. A shorted pass path without independent current limiting instead gives a prospective 0.965 A and 530.702 W in the 570 Ω feed. These values assume a stiff source; real source impedance and fault disconnection are not yet defined.

\[P_{\mathrm{pass,limited}}=[550-(0.03)(570)](0.03)=15.987\ \mathrm{W}.\]

\[I_{\mathrm{short}}=\frac{550}{570}=0.9649\ \mathrm{A},\qquad P_{\mathrm{feed,short}}=\frac{550^2}{570}=530.702\ \mathrm{W}.\]

The resistor's flameproof construction is not proof of safe fault interruption. The floating LM317L candidate also needs protection against excessive input-to-output voltage; its 40 V differential limit does not permit direct connection between 550 V input and the preamp rail. Actual startup, shutdown, short-circuit and pass-short protection must be designed before native adoption.

Pass-device follow-up

The IXYS IXTP3N100D2 datasheet, DS100184E(4/17) was recovered from a distributor bundle after the official download returned 403. Its page 5, Figure 16 shows the 550 V / 30 mA diagnostic point below the DC boundary at a 75 °C case. This is a visual feasibility screen, not acceptance of the complete circuit. The older document and current product page differ in on-resistance; confirm the supplied-device revision before selection. Source receipt and open checks.

This normally-on device needs a different gate circuit from an enhancement transistor. Its tab is the drain and requires an insulated mounting design if attached to earthed metal. With a provisional 45 °C local ambient and 75 °C case ceiling, the limited-short heat requires:

\[R_{\theta,\mathrm{case\ to\ ambient}}\le\frac{75-45}{15.987}=1.877\ \mathrm{K/W}.\]

The insulating interface and heatsink must share that allowance. The datasheet's typical case-to-sink value does not qualify an insulating pad. A case temperature above 75 °C requires a new safe-operating-area check; current limiting and fault interruption remain unimplemented.

Next action and human check

Define one real series-pass circuit, verify its DC safe operating area, differential protection and fault disconnection, then repeat the coupled-load check with that circuit. Carry its actual heatsink, support and connection dimensions into the lower power enclosure. Keep the 150 °C film allocation and 1.082 V voltage margin conditional until their physical causes are bounded.

Human check: verify the exact resistor variant, mounting heat path, pass-device temperature and protection behavior on the protected prototype. Native schematics, four existing PCB drafts and functional-block interfaces remain unchanged by this study; three-board fit and drill access are still open.

Vocabulary key

Shunt clamp
A circuit that diverts current to limit voltage.
Series-pass circuit
A device and controller placed in the supply path to regulate its output.
Dropout
Loss of regulation when the available input voltage is too low.
Film temperature
Temperature of the resistor's internal resistive material.
DC safe operating area
Allowed continuous combinations of transistor voltage and current at a stated temperature.
Behavioral model
An equation representing intended behavior without reproducing every device mechanism.
FFT
A calculation that separates a waveform into frequency components.
Fundamental
The waveform component at the applied test frequency.
Harmonic distortion
Unwanted output at integer multiples of the test frequency.
Prospective fault current
Current calculated before protective interruption or other limiting effects are applied.
