Give the fault hold a positive reset threshold

Add a candidate 1 kΩ resistor between the hold comparator output and the main comparator positive input. Powered-monitor tests pass. Full startup remains unverified; no native circuit or PCB changes are adopted.

Problem and correction

The previous hold circuit pulled the threshold almost to ground. With comparator offset and leakage included, its calculated lower reset bound was −0.665 V. That does not guarantee release when the amplifier output returns to zero.

The added resistor forms a divider with the existing 10 kΩ reference-feed resistor. It holds the positive input above ground even when the hold comparator output is low. The main overvoltage detector remains connected; this change does not add a startup interval that disables detection.

Let \(V_R\) be the reference voltage, \(V_L\) the hold output voltage, \(R_I=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) the reference-feed resistor, \(R_R=1\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) the new resistor, and \(I_P\) the allocated positive-input and board leakage current.

\[V_+=\frac{V_R/R_I+V_L/R_R-I_P}{1/R_I+1/R_R}\]

With ideal low output and zero leakage:

\[V_+=\frac{2.5(1000)}{10000+1000}=0.22727\,\mathrm V\]

The sensing divider uses \(R_T=1.176\,\mathrm{M\Omega}\) and \(R_B=10.1\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\). With comparator offset \(V_{OS}\) and sense-input leakage \(I_S\):

\[V_{reset}=(V_++V_{OS})\left(1+\frac{R_T}{R_B}\right)+I_SR_T\]

The candidate shunt model now includes a generic forward-conduction diode. It limits the artificial negative monitor rail to about −0.520 V instead of −21.9 V, but cannot make invalid comparator startup states trustworthy. The valid-rail tests use no imposed reset source.

The TI comparator model expressly returns midsupply outside its allowed input or supply range. A 2.5 V input needs at least 4.5 V supply under the stated common-mode limit; use a conservative 5 V valid-rail threshold for the next test fixture. The model failure below that boundary is not proof that the physical circuit fails. The ATL431 model also excludes temperature and AC/loop qualification. Low-voltage bench test needed

Test the monitor separately before energizing the amplifier. This requires the actual comparator, reference, shunt, optocouplers and socket carriers; a current-limited DC bench supply; an adjustable 0–3 V sense stimulus; an oscilloscope; and a meter. The Mac recording interface cannot measure the DC startup sequence.

Use a low-voltage feed fixture to reproduce the declared monitor current range. Replace the high-voltage sensing divider with a scaled stimulus and about 10 kΩ source resistance. Measure voltage at the comparator input. Leave all mains wiring, tube supplies and high-voltage pass devices disconnected. These measurements qualify the control circuit only; they do not qualify high-voltage spacing, feed-resistor heating or full-amp protection.

  1. Record supply, reference, both comparator outputs, threshold input and LED current through power-up and power loss.
  2. Try fast and slow ramps, interrupted power and different initial hold states.
  3. With a valid rail, sweep the sense stimulus upward and downward; record trip, release, delay and repeated switching.
  4. Compare actual thresholds and current with the allocated limits; repeat at the intended temperature limits using suitable equipment.

The full amplifier must also establish a valid monitor and reference before its output passes the minimum reset bound of 25.565 V. That ordering is not yet proved. Exact fixture parts, source calibration and acceptance timing must be specified before a hardware test is credited.

Sources and vocabulary

TI comparator limits; preserved comparator model; preserved reference model; HD-011 baseline.

Reset floor
A positive threshold below which a held fault must release within the declared limits.
Offset
The small input-voltage error of a comparator.
Leakage
Small unintended current through a device or circuit board.
Common-mode limit
The allowed input-voltage range relative to the comparator supply.
Imposed output
A test voltage supplied externally instead of produced by the amplifier.
Source resistance
The resistance seen between the stimulus source and its load.
Carrier
A removable adapter holding a surface-mount IC.
Endpoint combination
A calculation using a selected minimum or maximum for each declared input.
