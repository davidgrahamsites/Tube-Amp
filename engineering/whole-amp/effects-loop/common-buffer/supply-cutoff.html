PA340CC supply, mute and cooling

CB-007 · 28 November 2026. Hold three-board placement. A discrete 310 V regulator drove the modeled return. Independent cutoff contained a shorted pass transistor at 440 V with one reservoir capacitor open, under a 2 ms trip assumption. An abrupt supply fault still reached the mixer before either mute could open. Neither the present auxiliary winding nor the removable heat path meets the evidence needed for placement. CB-006 load and device limits.

Supply circuit under test

Feed this branch from the raw positive supply. Use 340 V at the ripple trough and 440 V at the crest as design inputs. The actual transformer, cabinet and fault maximum remain unmeasured. The project's separate 550 V cold-load diagnostic is also tested below. Keep the existing raw supply, this regulator and the external pedal jack on distinct nets.

  1. Route raw B+ through two 910 Ω resistors, an independent normally open AQV256HA, and an 8.2 kΩ precharge string to the pass drain. A second AQV256HA bridges only the precharge string. Sense raw B+ minus the protected rail through matched high-voltage dividers. Close the bypass below 140 V difference and open above 160 V. Loss of control opens both devices. The fixed 1.82 kΩ always limits fault current. Keep all bulk storage after the pass stage. A charged capacitor wired around this impedance invalidates the fault result.
  2. Use a Littelfuse IXTH30N60L2 source follower. Connect gate pull-up to drain through 100 kΩ in voltage-rated sections. Fit a 220 Ω gate stopper and a 12 V gate-source clamp, cathode to gate. Connect a 33 Ω sense resistor from source to protected rail. A floating 2N3904 senses across it, emitter to the rail and collector to the gate-drive node. Current limit varies with temperature and transistor gain. The gate pull-up also supplies a small bypass current.
  3. Feed the OPA197ID noninverting input through 306 kΩ from the protected rail and 2.49 kΩ to ground. Use three 102 kΩ sections. Connect its inverting input through 1 kΩ to a 2.5 V reference. Fit 499 kΩ feedback in parallel with a series 100 nF and 100 kΩ. The amplifier drives a VO617A-3 LED through 1.5 kΩ. Its floating collector pulls the pass gate toward its source as rail voltage rises. Use a dedicated regulator reference. The trip reference, divider, latch and series disconnect have separate electrical paths.
  4. Fit two independent Nichicon UPT2W220MHD 22 µF, 450 V reservoirs in parallel at the protected node. Return the PA340 bypass directly to that node and its ground. Provide a 360 kΩ bleeder in two 180 kΩ sections. The trial includes two 604 µA leakage allowances at 310 V. The series sheet quotes 604 µA per capacitor at its specified test condition. Hot leakage, low-temperature capacitance, ESR, aging and local bypass layout need verification.

The nominal divider gives \(2.5(1+306000/2490)=309.73\,\mathrm V\). The first 90/110 V bypass thresholds left the 8.2 kΩ in circuit around 400–420 V. A 100 ms load pulse at 400 V gave 302.14 V, but a sustained 8 mA load drove the rail to 283.50 V. With the bypass closed, the sustained test stayed above 309.59 V at all five 400–440 V troughs. Use the revised 140/160 V thresholds. In the conservative 52.8 µF startup envelope the bypass closes at 1.14 s at 340 V, 1.47 s at 390 V and 1.83 s at 440 V. It reaches 310 V in all nine input cases. The startup envelope assumes a monotonic rise; probe real hysteresis and relay delay through ripple and falling line. With the bypass stuck open at 340 V the rail stalls at 269.64 V. The startup timeout must trip. These are source/current-envelope calculations. Startup and fault receipt.

Independent overvoltage cutoff

Power a TI LM2903BIDR from the supervised +5 V control rail. Bias a separate LM4040AIM3-2.5/NOPB from +5 V through 2.49 kΩ. Feed its comparator positive input with this reference. Feed the negative input from the actual PA340 supply pins using four 82.0 kΩ series resistors over 2.49 kΩ. At overvoltage the open collector pulls the asynchronous clear of an SN74HC74N low. Tie its data and inactive preset high. A supervised, debounced rising arm edge clocks the latch. Use a pull-down on the AQV LED transistor drive. Gate its LED with control power valid and latch Q. A 470 Ω LED resistor supplies about 6–9 mA over the assumed +5 V window. The latch and regulator amplifier must never share a sense divider or reference component.

Clear on POR, missing control power, either missing analog rail, overtemperature or trip. Use the project’s signed rail monitors and a TL7705A-class supply supervisor. Refuse rearm until the protected rail is below 60 V and the operator releases and presses ARM. Require at least 30 s of healthy control power after startup or any trip before accepting a fresh edge. A second LTC6994-1 timer with divider 2097152 and 787 kΩ gives about 33.0 s nominal. Verify its tolerance and reset at power interruption. A persistent cold short adds at most 5.09 J per modeled attempt, about 0.17 W averaged over 30 s. This limits repeated attempts; qualify repetitive SOA and stop to find the short. Allow a zero-volt output during startup. Trip if it is still below 60 V after 1 s or below 300 V after 4 s. After entry into the operating window, drop out below 295 V or above 320 V. The separate hard OV comparator still trips if normal-window logic fails. Asynchronous clear overrides arm. Loss of each rail, held arm, fresh rearm and interrupted qualification are exercised at the logic interface. Check actual POR, back-power through comparator inputs, open reference, stuck transistor and power-down pin states on the populated control circuit. Sequence tests.

The 32 trip corners include ±0.9% reference, ±0.4% per divider arm, ±4 mV offset and ±62.5 nA input current. The comparator threshold is 325.68–338.03 V. Allocate 2 ms from threshold crossing to interrupted current, including sensing, logic, driver, optical switch and hot leakage. The 0.2 ms switch data-sheet maximum applies under its test conditions. Verify total delay at the PA340 pins. Prevent control-side and harness backfeed, and prove trip independently with the regulator command held on.

The archived TI LM2903B comparator model ran with the actual divider, a 1 nF sense filter, a 4.7 kΩ pullup and 100 pF output load. The high-threshold run pulled clear low 15.8 µs after the filtered sense crossed reference. With a 1.9 ms actuator surrogate the rail peaked at 343.53 V. A 10 ms actuator reached 365.25 V. An open upper sense string prevented hard trip: the rail reached 402.20 V by 40 ms with the normal-window channel deliberately absent. The latch and disconnect use behavioral elements. Worst-case offset and timing remain covered by the separate corner calculation. Comparator transients.

For a shorted pass, the fastest rise uses zero downstream load, zero switch resistance and minimum fixed resistance. With one 22 µF part open, its partner at −20% and the latest threshold, \(V_{pk}=440-(440-338.028)e^{-0.002/(1729\,\times\,17.6\,10^{-6})}=344.51\,\mathrm V\). The delay to the PA340 350 V absolute maximum is 3.80 ms. This is a fault ceiling. Normal operation stays near 310 V.

Default mute and startup

Keep the external return jack on the low-voltage OPA134 side. Silence that input during qualification. Keep two full-voltage series blocking capacitors, the 3.3 kΩ output resistor and permanent 1 MΩ bleeders on the common and receiver sides. With no control power, connect the isolated common to 10 kΩ and disconnect it from the internal mixer. To enter PLAY, require a 300–320 V rail, valid signed analog rails, PA output within 3 V of half rail, and common voltage within ±0.35 V for at least 250 ms. Restart the interval if either limit fails. Latch PLAY so normal audio does not retrigger the silence detector. All fault clears take priority.

Sense the common through 1 MΩ in voltage-rated sections into an OPA197 summing input at 2.5 V. Balance it with 1 MΩ to +5 V and return 1 MΩ feedback with antiparallel small-signal clamps. Around zero the output is approximately \(2.5-V_{common}\). Buffer the half-rail comparison with separate high-value dividers. Include clamp recovery, resistor errors and 2.5 µA bias injection in the final window test. The play simulation includes equivalent port and midpoint loads. A candidate LTC6994-1 rising-edge timer with divider 262144 and 54.9 kΩ gives 288 ms nominal. Use the verified minimum when checking the 250 ms requirement. Timer data sheet.

With the CB-006 Apex and TI models, all four starts from zero supply stopped at time zero. The models report a singular matrix or timestep failure. In three additional runs the starting operating point has ±15 V present and the PA at 100 V. A 0.5 s rise to 310 V first permits sustained release at about 1.99 s. A 20 ms rise permits it at 1.78 s and produces 98.56 V at the disconnected precharge contact. A shorted 47 µF gain capacitor leaves PA output at 307.55 V. The common eventually reads near zero, but the midpoint interlock correctly withholds PLAY for all 5 s. These runs exercise powered recovery. Cold start, partially discharged restart and delayed negative rail still require physical testing. Seven startup receipts.

Auxiliary power and actual parts

The original 72-case rectifier study uses a provisional 0.25 A per half-winding screen. Rerun all 72 cases with each mute. Both runs reserve another 15 mA on each analog rail. The mechanical circuit adds 132.2 mA on +12 V, including a cold coil allowance of 116.2 mA, and 28 mA on +5 V. All 72 exceed the winding screen. Maximum winding RMS current is 0.457 A. The four regulators dissipate up to 5.71 W on the conditional model. The optical mute adds 16 mA on +12 V and 58 mA on +5 V. Maximum winding RMS is 0.382 A and regulator heat is 5.50 W. Forty-eight exceed winding current and 56 have at least one flag. Check the transformer, the +5 V stage and heat before adopting either mute. Mechanical supply run. Optical supply run.

Cooling and placement decision

The CB-006 hot-port-short budget is 2.844 W, including reserve. With 70 °C local air and an 85 °C PA340 case limit, the entire case-to-air path must be below \((85-70)/2.844=5.27\,\mathrm{K/W}\). Trial a dedicated vertical Boyd 530002B02500G extrusion, 63.50 mm high, about 41.9 × 25.4 mm in section. The catalog lists 2.6 K/W at a 75 °C rise. Use its low-power curve and test orientation in the closed cabinet. Solder the PA340 ground tab to a copper spreader on its removable carrier. Clamp the spreader to the extrusion through a qualified thermal interface. Keep the heat path independent of socket contacts. Preserve the project's signal-ground/chassis bonding scheme. Do not rely on anodizing for electrical isolation. Boyd catalog, printed page 56. Apex thermal and SOA limits.

Using 4 K/W installed sink-to-air and 1 K/W case-to-sink gives 80.28 °C at full output and 84.22 °C in the short. Only 0.78 °C remains in the short case. Either 7 K/W at the fins or 4 K/W at the joint raises the calculated case to 92.75 °C. The 79 °C nominal trip can inhibit normal full output at 80.28 °C. Reject the 70 °C internal-air mounting target for continuous full output. Carry forward an external-wall sink exposed to at most 60 °C effective air. With the same 5 K/W path it gives 70.28 °C full and 74.22 °C short. Check the minimum actual trip threshold against those values and the maximum against the case limit. Measure wall conduction, radiant heating, touch access and ground isolation. These are installed-path allocations. The actual carrier, spring tank, cabinet, resistor heat and adjacent power devices have not been tested.

Mount a TI TMP235A2DBZR near the package top or a thermally characterized case contact. Use a 79 °C nominal trip (1.29 V) and a low plausibility threshold to catch an open sensor wire pulled to ground. Clear both the run latch and the HV latch. The datasheet family limit is ±2.5 °C over temperature. Budget another 0.5 °C for the trip circuit; measure case-to-sensor gradients separately. With an assumed 1 J/K case, 8 J/K sink, degraded joint and fins, and 0.2 s residual heat decay, the 70 °C initial-air test with 1 s sensor lag peaks at 82.41 °C. At 8 s lag it reaches 85.16 °C. A detached sensor reading the sink permits 93.91 °C. Repeating the same degraded joint and fins from 60 °C gives 82.15 °C at 1 s lag and 83.17 °C at 8 s. The detached sensor never trips in 300 s and the case reaches 91.08 °C. Verify the peak directly on the package top. A thermal sensor cannot qualify a loose joint. TI sensor. Thermal results.

Placement stays on hold. Assign the raw feed, reservoirs and live pass mount to the power-board study. Keep the PA carrier, its compensation and its local sensing at the internal return. Keep safety control and mute current returns away from the dry and spring inputs. Before capture, establish the actual raw maximum, choose an auxiliary source with winding and regulator margin, prove the mute lead and off-state transfer, and measure both package tops and the installed case-to-air path. Check exact carriers, resistor pulse ratings, creepage, service access and received parts. These are functional allocations for the existing three boards.

Two independent Claude reviews flagged bypass deadlock, repeated short stress, hot sink rating and mute timing. The follow-up produced the sustained-load tests, revised bypass thresholds, minimum reset interval and external-wall cooling target. The thermal integration already included a −3 °C sensor error; adding it a second time would double-count it. The circuit has one PA340. The source-drop and thermal checks here use its actual modeled branch; Claude's two-chip assumption was discarded. No purchase, bench or fabrication evidence was available.

The receipt audit independently checks the fault-rise equation, both mute results, compensation, all 144 auxiliary cases and the original native design. Audit receipt.

Vocabulary key

Phase margin
Remaining phase shift to oscillation at the frequency where loop gain reaches one.
PhotoMOS
A solid-state switch controlled by an isolated light-emitting diode.
NC and NO
Normally closed and normally open, with drive power removed.
CTR
Optocoupler output current divided by LED input current.
SOA
Safe operating area for a transistor at a specified voltage, current, temperature and duration.
POR
Power-on reset.
THD
Total harmonic distortion relative to the fundamental tone.
ESR
Equivalent series resistance of a capacitor.
Rail
A power-supply connection.
