Effects-return recovery under load

CB-006 · 27 November 2026. Keep placement on hold. A PA340CC recovery stage drove the CB-005 modeled load at 135.54 V peak and 0.292% total harmonic distortion (THD) at 20 kHz. Supply sag, fault isolation and case cooling set the next design limits. All results below are simulations or calculations.

Candidate circuit

Use the same buffered path in effects and bypass. The external return jack belongs on the low-voltage side. The internal recovery output drives the dry mixer and spring-send divider. The test fixture uses FX_RETURN as the downstream node name. Native J4 currently shares that name. Split the internal and jack nets before any capture. Preserve polarity through both gain stages.

  1. Model a socketed TI OPA134PA on ±15 V. Use 10 kΩ to ground and 40.2 kΩ feedback for gain 5.02. Feed two series 1 µF blocking capacitors through two 24 kΩ resistors. Bias the PA340 input with 470 kΩ to the 310 V rail and 470 kΩ to ground.
  2. Use 48 kΩ feedback in two 24 kΩ sections. Return the gain leg through 2 kΩ and 47 µF to ground. AC gain approaches 25. DC gain approaches one. Fit 4.7 pF C0G across the compensation pins, rated for the full rail. Ideal midband gain including the input divider is \(5.02(235/283)25=104.21\) before output loading.
  3. Place 3.3 kΩ outside the feedback loop. Couple the internal output through two series 2.2 µF film capacitors. Provide a 1 MΩ bleed from the load side to ground. Each blocking capacitor must withstand the full applicable voltage if its partner shorts. Individual leakage and discharge remain open. The 47 µF gain-leg capacitor also sees the DC midpoint.
  4. Trial R601 at 1.37 MΩ with R602 at 100 kΩ. The spring input reached 9.27 V peak at nominal overload against its 10 V design limit. An earlier trial with the original 1 MΩ exceeded that limit. At equal recovery output the spring excitation scales by \(11/14.7=0.748\), a 2.52 dB reduction. Recalibrate wet level and tank drive with the complete spring circuit.

The pass limits are ±0.5 dB relative to the target and 1% THD, harmonics 2–10. The 135 V target represents overload. Simulations use the manufacturer electrical models, the modeled mixer, generic tubes and the transformer fixture. The spring wet source is silent. The high-frequency tests omit physical wiring, signal-source variation, supply startup and actual case temperature. A source-impedance trial with 310 V and the same reservoir passed. The 279 V trial fell to 276.01 V at the chip. Require an operating window from the chosen power supply at both mains settings.

The high-voltage loop had one descending unity-gain crossing in each of five AC probes. Calculated phase margin was 85.5–92.2° for the sampled conditions, including 1 pF across feedback and 2 nF external load in the last case. The probe injects a series voltage at the low-impedance feedback pickoff. Verify the approximation, full device spread and carrier parasitics on the final circuit. The required sine slew rate is \(2\pi(20\,000)(135)=16.96\) V/µs. Apex specifies 32 V/µs typical at 4.7 pF.

Heat and fault checks

At 320 V and model temperature 85 °C, the loaded 20 kHz sine dissipates 1.255 W in the PA340. A 0.1 Ω internal port short dissipates 2.044 W in the chip and 2.989 W in the isolation resistor. Peak resistor current is 42.59 mA. The chip supplies 45.40 mA including feedback. The resistor loss was calculated from its own voltage waveform. Model temperature is an imposed electrical parameter.

With 70 °C ambient and an 85 °C case limit the allowable rise is 15 °C. Add a conservative 0.800 W reserve to each simulated loss, using the entire 320 V × 2.5 mA maximum quiescent allowance. This deliberately counts some idle loss twice. The design budgets require case-to-air resistance below \(15/2.055=7.30\) K/W in overload and \(15/2.844=5.27\) K/W in the port short. At an 85 °C case, the latter gives \(85+8.9(2.844)=110.3\) °C junction. A timer that interrupts the short can change this budget only after its worst clearing time is verified.

Apex's typical 27 K/W junction-to-air figure uses 2 oz copper with 1,000 mm² topside and 2,500 mm² backside copper. The current carrier and three-board assembly have no equivalent verified heat path. Verify the power trajectory against figure 15 at the measured case temperature and the board's actual thermal resistance. Keep the 3.3 kΩ resistor heat separate from the chip budget.

  • Supply fault. The modeled rail is 310 V. A pass-device short to the 440 V raw supply exceeds the PA340 absolute maximum by 90 V. Require an independent means to disconnect or bound the rail under this fault. Qualify stored energy, trip tolerance, response time, restart and loss of either low-voltage rail. A 320 V DC fault across 3.3 kΩ would dissipate 31.0 W. A continuous short at the AC-coupled output and a sustained DC fault have different stresses.
  • Jack and input. Keep the external jack on the low-voltage side. The final design uses 48 kΩ in two sections between the OPA134 output and the coupling pair. A forced 320 V step at the far side bounds initial current to about 6.67 mA into a grounded low side. With the midpoint divider intact, a 160 V step through its 235 kΩ source gives 0.57 mA. Check resistor pulses, coupling-cap shorts, rail injection and the unpowered OPA output. A permissible sink path into either disabled rail is still required.
  • Power sequencing. The effective 1.1 µF output capacitor can store 14.08 mJ at 160 V. Evaluate an independently muted internal return with a default 10 kΩ precharge load. For a prescribed 160 V step through 3.3 kΩ plus 10 kΩ, peak current is 12.03 mA and the load decays below 1 V in 70.1 ms. This isolated RC calculation does not establish a safe relay delay. Monitor actual rail and output conditions through power-up, shutdown, brownout and contact bounce. Verify the external jack across every switching state.

Disposition and reproduction

Keep the PA340CC as the signal candidate. Before placement, qualify a dedicated bounded supply, fault shutdown and mute, the removable heat path and the actual connected load. Repeat with the active wet circuit, full controls, real source impedance and wiring capacitance. No schematic, PCB or selected BOM adopts this stage.

Claude independently reviewed the initial circuit values. Its thermal and power-sequencing concerns led to the hot-short test, finite-source test and 48 kΩ input revision. The review assumed a 1 kΩ path to a stiff midpoint and called the PA340 a SOIC. The intact divider and manufacturer DDPAK drawing corrected those assumptions. Use the applicable voltage and insulation rules for the actual assembly. The supplier's pin pitch alone does not establish clearance or creepage.

The independent receipt check reconstructed all 26 case decks, checked final times and confirmed that the four native boards and 18 schematic files match the CB-005 commit. Audit receipt.

Reproduce with KiCad Python. Store the unmodified PA340 archive and PA341 archive under ~/.cache/renegade/apex/ or set RENEGADE_APEX_MODELS. The circuit factory checks each archive hash. Execute normal and short cases, feedback and supply tests, then thermal and fault arithmetic. Machine results retain the initial and revised designs.
