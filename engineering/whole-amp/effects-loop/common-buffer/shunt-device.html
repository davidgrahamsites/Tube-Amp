Effects-return mute devices

The effects return drives the mixer through the two delay windings. A normally-on pair of transistors shorts the mixer input during a fault. In PLAY, the gates hold both transistors off. Charge stored in an open transistor can change the treble response. Releasing its gate can inject an audible pulse. The quiet-signal gain limit is ±0.50 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. The switching-pulse limit is 0.70 V. Previous network and cabinet fit kit.

CB-012 · Opposed silicon MOSFETs

Reject the trial that connects the common sources of two IXTP3N50D2 devices to −12 V through 47 kΩ. The IXYS table gives typical output capacitance of 102 pF at 25 V. Extrapolating a single square-root law to zero gives \(102\sqrt{26}=520\,\mathrm{pF}\). Figure 13 reads about 2,350 pF near zero. Reverse-transfer capacitance rises from 24 pF to about 1,000 pF. These are typical readings at 1 MHz. The table specifies −10 V gate bias. The figure does not give a separate gate bias. Claude independently reviewed the larger IXTP6N50D2 and reported the same low-voltage rise. IXYS 3N50D2, table and figure 13.

Linearly interpolate the 14 capacitance readings. Integrate each segment as \(Q(V)=\int_0^V C(u)\,du\). Split the charge into gate–drain and source–drain terms. Hold the endpoint capacitance outside the graph. Each of the 14 AC calibration points reproduces both input columns within 0.5 pF. Sweep two gate-axis assumptions and three charge multipliers, 0.75 to 1.25. These multipliers are engineering sensitivities. The manufacturer gives no production charge tolerance. CB-013 · Normally-on silicon-carbide JFETs

The UJ3N065080K3S is a 650 V normally-on junction field-effect transistor in a TO-247-3 package. Two opposed devices share their sources. A floating driver applies −18 V in PLAY and 0 V in MUTE. The June 2025 sheet specifies 95 mΩ maximum at zero gate voltage, 10 A and 25 °C. It gives 130 mΩ typical at 175 °C. The gate threshold spans −14 to −6 V at 20 mA. The absolute negative gate limit is −20 V. The previous 6.2 V gate clamp cannot hold this device off. onsemi device limits and package.

Claude recovered UnitedSiC model 3.1 from the manufacturer's archived site and checked its pin order. The copy supplied with the manufacturer's symbol in a second archive is byte-identical. The model accepts drain, gate, source. The adapter preserves the equations and translates nested parameter braces, voltage limiting and natural logarithms for ngspice. The channel model omits thermal feedback and blocking leakage. Its threshold has no temperature coefficient. The 10 MΩ gate–drain resistance affects the floating operating point. The preliminary model must be checked against a real pair near cutoff. Original model · Hash-checked adapter.

Use a 40.60 kΩ feedback arm with the CB-011 winding network. The model gives −0.48872 to +0.48410 dB over 32 quiet-signal corners. At the two AC extremes the 340 V raw supply test gives −0.1580 and +0.2041 dB at full level. THD is 0.555% and 0.829%. The lowest loaded PA supply is 309.13 V. These are steady 20 kHz results at the two selected extremes. Loaded audio and AC results.

Test a ±160 V step while silent and at four phases of a 135 V peak source. All 20 JFET transients reached the required stop time. Two needed a 1 ns step after the first attempt stopped early. Across both wiring cases the worst residual after 100 ns is 0.647 V. Peak shunt current is 6.075 A. Existing music reached about 145 V before closing. The pulse and receiver voltage refer to different instants. Driver input, gate, mixer and shunt current must be captured together on built hardware. Signed faults, loop resistance and truncated attempts.

The matched model produces at most 0.247 V on gate release to MUTE. Changing only the ground-side device to −6 V or −14 V channel threshold with an accompanying change in its added Miller capacitance raises the worst pulse to 3.926 V. With both thresholds near −10 V, a ±0.25 V difference and ±10% change in this capacitance branch give 0.456 V. That leaves 0.244 V against the 0.70 V limit. The datasheet does not specify pair matching. Gate-charge variation, absolute threshold, leakage, driver impedance and isolation capacitance still matter. The capacitance change applies to the separate gate–drain network; the intrinsic JFET capacitance retains its original equation. 21 gate transitions with the 20 ns source.

Vocabulary key

Terms used in this report. See the full key for related terms.

AC
Alternating current; current that changes direction.
Bias
A steady voltage or current that sets a device operating point.
Capacitance
The ratio of stored charge to voltage; measured in farads (F).
Capacitor
A component that stores charge and electrical energy.
Clearance
The shortest distance through air between conductive parts.
Feedback
A signal returned from an output to an earlier stage to control gain or response.
Ground
A named circuit reference; it is not automatically protective earth.
Harness
A grouped set of wires, contacts and connectors.
Impedance
Opposition to alternating current, including resistance and frequency-dependent effects; measured in ohms (Ω).
Loop resistance
The total resistance of the supply and return conductors, contacts and terminations.
Qualification
Checks that show a design meets stated limits under stated conditions.
Tolerance
The permitted variation from a specified value.
Winding
A coil of insulated wire in a transformer or inductor.
Gate
A checkpoint that requires specified evidence before the next activity.
Thermal
Related to heat or temperature.
THD
Total harmonic distortion; harmonic content relative to the fundamental, using the stated harmonic range.
