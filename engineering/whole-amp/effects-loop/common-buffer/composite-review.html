CB-002 · 26 November 2026 · Engineering experiment

A discrete high-voltage recovery stage

The composite circuit is feasible at the nominal operating point, but is not ready for selection or PCB integration. The transistor-and-op-amp current sink reproduces about 135 V peak at 1 kHz. At 20 kHz, full-swing distortion exceeds the 1% screen. Device-sensitivity initialization, gate protection, capacitance and heat remain unresolved.

Purpose and circuit

Keep the approved common buffered path in both loop and bypass modes. Investigate a cheaper recovery stage than the three-OPA462 reference without silently imposing the rejected 10/12 V ceiling. The 135 V peak stress target comes from the earlier overload experiment; it is not the normal clean requirement or a verified all-setting maximum.

The low-voltage error amplifier controls a common-source high-voltage transistor. A source follower drives the DC-blocking capacitor and load. Feedback regulates the follower near 200 V DC with nominal AC gain 100. The candidate uses a second op-amp and third transistor to regulate sink current; its 1 V reference is still ideal.

Compensation connects the error-amplifier output to its inverting input through 10 pF, with 100 kΩ from the input source. A 1 pF feedback-divider capacitance is included. These small values require layout and socket-parasitic review; they are not final component selections.

Model boundaries

Use the archived, hash-checked TI OPAx134 macro-model. The three transistors use an authored square-law model, not a manufacturer model. It matches selected threshold and zero-gate-current parameters but omits breakdown, body-diode behavior, temperature and nonlinear capacitance. It does not establish full LND150 behavior. Supplies and references are stiff. The load is a standalone assumption; the actual spring/phase-inverter interface must be tested separately.

The Microchip LND150/LND250 datasheet supplies the voltage, parameter and typical thermal reference. Its 500 V breakdown rating and ±20 V gate limit do not validate this model or circuit. No part is selected, ordered or claimed available from a qualified Chinese source in this pass.

Current and heat set the next design limits

For a sinusoid across parallel resistance and capacitance, the necessary output-current amplitude is:

\[I_{\mathrm{pk}}=V_{\mathrm{pk}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^2+(2\pi fC)^2}\]

At \(V_{\mathrm{pk}}=135\,\mathrm{V}\), \(f=20\,\mathrm{kHz}\), and \(R=340\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\): 100 pF needs 1.742 mA; 500 pF needs 8.492 mA. For an ideal 2 mA sink, the no-cutoff capacitance ceiling is:

\[C_{\max}=\frac{\sqrt{(I_Q/V_{\mathrm{pk}})^2-(1/R)^2}}{2\pi f}=115.546\,\mathrm{pF}\]

This is a necessary bound, not a distortion guarantee; transistor displacement current, tolerances and useful margin reduce the available allowance. It is too tight to approve an unspecified inter-board harness.

The servo-sink 20 kHz case dissipates about 0.407 W average in the follower. With the assumed 70°C ambient and typical 132°C/W thermal resistance:

\[T_J\approx T_A+P\theta_{JA}=70+0.40703(132)=123.73^\circ\mathrm{C}\]

That leaves only about 1.27°C below the chosen 125°C design ceiling. The typical thermal estimate is not a guaranteed limit; the package is not approved. The drain resistor reaches about 1.10 W instantaneous in this case; continuous, pulse and voltage ratings require separate selection. Do not infer a resistor wattage from average dissipation alone.

Next action

Keep the composite topology as a candidate. Select a supported transistor model and a follower/current-sink package with adequate hot dissipation and safe operating area; add gate protection and bound the real references. Resolve invalid DC starts and 20 kHz distortion, then test the complete common-loop, spring mix and fault paths. Only then assign connector voltage domains, route the interface and update populated chassis geometry. No tube, functional block, cabinet mount or active PCB changed.

Output current and heat — CB-004

Reserve a heat path before choosing a larger output device. This calculation checks an ideal follower and constant-current sink at a 400 V supply and 200 V output bias. It does not select a transistor or approve the circuit. The 135 V peak signal at 20 kHz is an overload stress. It is not the normal clean 25 W requirement. The 100, 250 and 500 pF loads are trial values, not measured cable capacitances.

Load-current calculation

Let \(A\) be signal peak voltage, \(R\) be load resistance, \(C\) be load capacitance and \(f\) be frequency. Resistive and capacitive sine currents differ in phase by 90 degrees. Add their squared peaks.

\[I_{L,pk}=\sqrt{(A/R)^2+(2\pi f C A)^2}\]

At \(A=135\,\mathrm{V}\), \(R=340\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\), \(C=500\,\mathrm{pF}\) and \(f=20\,\mathrm{kHz}\), \(I_{L,pk}=8.4916\,\mathrm{mA}\). A 13 mA trial bias exceeds this load-only floor by a factor of 1.531. Internal feedback current, transistor capacitance and bias tolerance are excluded. This ratio is not a selected current margin.

Standing heat

At zero signal, each device has 200 V across it and carries the trial bias current \(I_Q\).

\[P_F=P_S=200\,\mathrm{V}\times13\,\mathrm{mA}=2.6\,\mathrm{W},\qquad P_{pair}=5.2\,\mathrm{W}\]

The ideal signal-current integration gives follower mean power \(P_F=(400-200)I_Q-A^2/(2R)=2.5732\,\mathrm{W}\) and sink mean power \(P_S=200I_Q=2.6\,\mathrm{W}\). Use the higher standing losses for this steady thermal screen. These means do not qualify instantaneous stress, faults or continuous linear operation.

Shared heat-sink limit

Assume 70°C local air, a 125°C junction design limit and 2°C/W per insulating mounting interface. These are design assumptions. For two identical devices on one sink, the upper calculated sink-to-air resistance is:

\[\theta_{SA}\leq\frac{125-70-2.6(\theta_{JC}+2)}{5.2}\;\mathrm{^{\circ}C/W}\]

Find the external current requirement

Use the prior 1 A AC port probe to obtain complex impedance. At 20 kHz the modeled return presents 276.990 − j176.520 kΩ. Its admittance is 2.5675 + j1.6362 µS. For a sine of peak voltage A, add the trial capacitance C in parallel.

\[ I_{pk}=A\left|Y+j2\pi f C\right| \]

At 135 V peak the linearized port requires 0.411, 1.948 and 8.710 mA peak with 0, 100 and 500 pF added. At 100 pF the external load alone uses 97.4% of the nominal 2 mA bias on this calculation. The 2 mA crossing occurs at 103.09 pF. This diagnostic excludes feedback-divider current, device capacitances, nonlinear load behavior and tolerances. Use the transient results above to assess signal reproduction. Record total connected signal length, including service loops. For a dimensional reference, 0.30 m of Belden 8216 at its nominal 101 pF/m contributes 30.3 pF before connector and board capacitance. Measure the disconnected harness conductor to its installed shield/return and account separately for board capacitance before assigning a limit. Cable selection, insulation, temperature and crimp fit remain open. Belden 8216 data sheet.

The nominal spring-send divider delivers about \(135\times100/(1000+100)=12.27\) V peak at full recovery swing. The native U10 design limit is 10 V peak. Verify spring excitation and overload behavior when recalibrating the mixer. The recovery output and the internal spring-send input require separate acceptance checks.

Screen replacement devices

Microchip rates the DN2540 at 400 V. The existing 440 V high-supply experiment exceeds this by 40 V if the full rail appears across one device during startup or a fault. Exclude it as an unprotected full-rail replacement. Microchip DN2540 data sheet.

The Littelfuse IXTP3N50D2 merits a separate driver study. It has a 500 V rating, a drain-connected mounting tab and published DC safe-operating-area curves at 25 and 75 °C case temperature. Its typical input capacitance is 1,070 pF at 25 V drain-source voltage, −10 V gate-source voltage and 1 MHz. The CB-002 follower uses 9 pF gate-source and 1 pF gate-drain. Recalculate gate drive and compensation with voltage-dependent capacitances and check the insulated mounting path before reserving this package. Littelfuse data sheet, pages 1–2 and figures 15–16.
