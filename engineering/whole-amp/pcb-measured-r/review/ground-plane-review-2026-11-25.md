# Ground-plane and return-path review — November25

User asks: “the board should have one side with a large ground pour, right?” This is a design clarification, not approval of a particular finished copper geometry.

Engineering direction: for two-layer low-voltage audio/control regions, reserve B.Cu for a broad, substantially continuous ground reference, minimizing signal routing that cuts it into strips. Extra top-side ground copper must connect deliberately to that reference. Keep functional blocks together by placement and local return loops; do not automatically cut a moat around each block. Traces must not cross a return-plane gap without a designed return path. Remove floating islands, check narrow necks/current density and stitch connected regions where necessary.

For this tube amplifier, distinguish circuit0V from protective earth/chassis bonding. Map rectifier/reservoir charging, heater, relay and power-stage returns so their shared impedance does not contaminate input/reverb recovery references. The power/tube regions require voltage-appropriate clearances, creepage and copper keepouts; a blanket entire-board GND fill can violate those. The PCB ground plane never replaces the protective-earth chassis bond. A high-impedance input may also need a local capacitance/feedback keepout justified by its device requirements. These are application-specific engineering constraints, not copied claims of qualification from generic analog guidance.

Primary grounding guidance: [Analog Devices, Staying Well Grounded](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/analog-dialogue/articles/staying-well-grounded.html) explains the low resistance/inductance benefits of a large plane and the errors caused by shared high-current returns. [ADI mixed-signal grounding FAQ](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/faqs/faq_dds_digital_and_analog_gnd_planes.html) supports a common low-impedance plane and checking each device's requirements. These support the starting architecture; they do not qualify our high-voltage spacing, current budget or socket layout.

Native audit at5e7f774: Main, Control, Power and Reverb each contain zero KiCad zone nodes. No ground pour has been implemented. Even the routed Reverb draft therefore lacks this design step. Plane creation/refill, island and return-path inspection, current/clearance checks, strict DRC and signal integrity/bench verification remain open. No native board changed in this audit. Do not infer grounding completion from a routed net or zero DRC findings.

Next physical implementation: use the already completed T03 logical bond contract and finish its physical/interboard return mapping, then add each board's zone/keepout definitions around qualified placement and voltage domains. Review the resulting filled copper, not just its polygon outline. Human check: follow each noisy current loop and each sensitive input return on the actual filled copper, verify chassis bonding, and inspect all return interruptions and high-voltage keepouts before fabrication.
