# Trace-width research and amplifier application
Owner: PCB integration. Reviewed 2026-11-25. Task: digest the supplied references, distinguish electrical sizing from fabrication tolerance, and inform existing routing requirements without changing functional blocks. This is research, not a completed current-capacity check.

## Source assessment
- [Autodesk, Edwin Robledo, 2021-10-21](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/trace-width/): useful distinction between default CAD widths and current/temperature-based sizing; also addresses parasitics and return paths. Apply current and voltage-drop sizing to power/returns. Its broad right-angle/reflection discussion must not be interpreted as an audio-band failure mechanism. Do not use solder buildup as a controlled copper cross-section or its generic manufacturing minima as today's fabrication contract.
- [SierraConnect, PoulomiG, 2026-02-04](https://sierraconnect.protoexpress.com/t/how-do-you-decide-the-trace-width-for-your-pcb-designs/3848): useful checklist of copper construction, board environment and temperature rise, with links to IPC-2152 guidance. Clarification: temperature rise is a difference above the local thermal baseline, not the baseline temperature itself. RMS current governs periodic resistive heating; separately check pulse magnitude, duration and protective clearing. Peak current alone does not describe both cases.
- [DYC, Trace Width vs Current](https://www.dyc-electronic.com/trace-width-vs-current-in-pcb-design/): useful resistance/heating explanation and distinction between legacy IPC-2221 calculations and IPC-2152 treatment of board environment. Its generic table and approximate rules do not identify enough assumptions for this amplifier's acceptance. Do not adopt a universal current-per-via value; barrel geometry, minimum plating, sharing and thermal conditions matter. Wider copper lowers resistance but does not imply proportional current capacity.
- User-pasted ALLPCB article, Adrian, 2025-08-27: the tolerance warning is useful. Its example of a 10% width reduction changing 2 A capacity to 1.5 A is unsupported by stated thermal conditions and is not our sizing rule. The claim that thicker copper generally improves etch precision is not accepted. Actual fabricator limits govern. Its fixed-length/thickness resistance example is consistent with R proportional to 1/width: losing 10% width raises resistance by 11.11%.
- [JLCPCB rigid-board capabilities](https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/Capabilities), checked 2026-11-25: publishes ±20% track-width tolerance. Two-layer minima are 0.10/0.10 mm width/space for 1 oz and 0.16/0.16 mm for 2 oz. These are manufacturing capabilities, not ampacity or high-voltage spacing. Use 20% possible narrowing provisionally; confirm the ordered process and minimum finished copper thickness before release. JLCPCB is an evidence example, not a placed order or final vendor commitment.

## Engineering disposition
The canonical margin policy is in [routing requirements](../routing-requirements.md). Engineering chooses a 1.5× finished-width target above the calculated minimum for power and return routes, with 2× preferred where it fits without compromising other constraints. This is project headroom, not an IPC mandate and not 1.5×/2× current capacity. Sensitive signal routes require individual parasitic review. All routes must still meet their calculated minimum.

For fractional negative fabrication tolerance f and engineering width multiplier M:

    drawn width >= M × required minimum finished width / (1 − f)

At f=0.20, merely preserving the minimum requires 1.25× drawn width. A 1.5× finished-width target requires 1.875× drawn width; a 2× target requires 2.5×. Example only: if a qualified calculation requires 1.00 mm finished copper, draw at least 1.875 mm, conveniently 2.00 mm; worst-case width is then 1.60 mm. This is not a 1 A or other current rating.

Use minimum finished thickness in the underlying thermal/drop calculation. Do not double-count tolerance if a validated sizing tool already includes it. Check positive width tolerance and the fabricator's finished spacing guarantee as well; widening cannot consume required HV clearance/creepage. Start with a 10 °C self-heating design target, then account separately for the local hot-cabinet baseline and component limits. That target remains subject to the actual thermal model and prototype measurements.

Prioritize heater, charging/rectifier, supply, speaker and shared-return paths wherever they actually traverse a board. Inspect every pad neck, via, thermal spoke and connector. Preserve the intended 45-degree/arc routing style. Do not enlarge high-impedance grids or feedback nodes indiscriminately. Actual currents, full-route drop, pulse/fault survival, final ground pours and populated chassis temperature remain unqualified.

### Independent checks and primary support

[Analog Devices' high-speed layout guide](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/analog-dialogue/articles/high-speed-printed-circuit-board-layout.html) supports reviewing return continuity, parasitic capacitance and feedback geometry. Its high-speed examples are not evidence of audible degradation in our amp. [TI SBOA237, sections3.6/4.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sboa237/sboa237.pdf) supports local bypassing, short feedback paths and appropriate audio grounding. Apply those layout principles without importing its HiFi performance targets or replacing our circuit. For copper material constants, use the [NBS copper tables](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/circ/nbscircular31e2.pdf): the calculations below approximate annealed copper at20°C with resistivity1.724e-8ohm·m and relative permeability1. They are illustrative physics checks, not measurements of ordered PCB foil.

- Skin-depth calculation: delta=sqrt(rho/(pi*f*mu0)). At20kHz, delta=467.3micrometres, much greater than nominal35/70micrometre foil; at1MHz it is66.1micrometres. Through-thickness skin effect is not the governing reason to widen ordinary audio traces here. This does not exclude geometry/proximity effects, MHz op-amp instability or RF coupling; actual fast-edge loops still need review.
- Resistance counterexample: a100mm-long,0.1mm-wide,35micrometre-thick conductor at20°C has R=0.49257ohm. In a simple ideal-source/10kohm-resistive-load divider, its attenuation is0.000428dB. This disproves a universal audible-loss claim based on width alone; it does not approve that geometry for power, shared returns, manufacturability or every signal node.
- Transmission-line scale: at an illustrative propagation speed1.5e8m/s,20kHz wavelength is7.5km; a0.1m board trace is about0.0048degrees long. Ordinary internal audio interconnects are analyzed as lumped circuits. Output-transformer/speaker load matching remains necessary and is a different problem. Fast switching edges and unwanted oscillation must be assessed at their actual frequencies.
- Check high-impedance capacitance explicitly: a simple100kohm source with100pF shunt gives a15.9kHz pole. This is an illustrative RC example, not an extracted board value. Extra copper or a plane placed indiscriminately can therefore harm treble or stability even where DC resistance is negligible.
