Widen the complete reverb supply paths

Both candidate supply rails now use 0.8 mm copper. The change covers 79 straight segments and four arcs, including pad exits. No component, hole, route centreline or functional block moves. Native DRC reports zero violations and zero open connections.

Current basis

The calculation rechecks current passive values, both rail shunts, both pots and all nine OPA2134 symbol units before recomputing the twelve retained load cases. The old current-budget contract's whole-source hash is historical; this run records current hashes and checks the model's actual electrical inputs instead of presenting the old receipt as fresh.

The ideal-source load model gives a maximum 69.725 mA per rail at 10 V peak output and 99.925 mA at the diagnostic 18 V peak excitation. Use 100 mA for this copper screen. These figures include six 5 mA quiescent allowances under the manufacturer's 25°C table conditions. Hot quiescent current, clipping, startup, clamp injection and faults remain outside the bound. The 18 V excitation is not an achievable linear-swing claim. TI OPAx134, SBOS058B, electrical table.

Construction and acceptance targets

Use the candidate construction contract: two outer layers, 35 µm nominal foil, required minimum 30 µm finished foil and 20 µm via barrels. Calculate at 70°C copper and 20% trace narrowing. Minimum finished thickness remains subject to fabricator acceptance.

Allocate 50 mV to each rail's tracks and vias. This is an engineering allocation, not the total supply-error allowance; pad, connector, harness and ground-return losses remain separate. Apply DEC-020's minimum 1.5× finished-width margin. Prefer 2× where routing clearance permits. Neither margin is a thermal or fault-current rating.

Calculation

Sum every segment and arc on each rail. Applying the full screen current to that sum conservatively overbounds track resistance loss for a branched, single-feed passive path; it does not identify each branch's actual current. Include all three vias on each rail using the requested minimum plating and a 1.76 mm maximum board length.

The former 0.3 mm rails fail the 50 mV screen. A 0.6 mm proposal fails the required width margin. Halving the assumed copper thickness also makes both 0.8 mm rails fail the drop limit. Width and thickness therefore remain coupled acceptance requirements.

Rejected trials and correction

A 1.0 mm straight-segment trial produced ten clearance violations. Its temporary export also lacked the library table, producing 41 configuration warnings; the table was restored before the accepted trial. No finding was waived and no clearance rule was weakened.

The first 0.8 mm generator pass left four +15 V arcs at 0.3 mm. Native DRC and the independent minimum-width check rejected it. The generator and preservation checker now handle arcs as well as straight segments. The final candidate retains every original arc centreline and changes only its width.

The candidate has added 0.8 mm rail rules alongside the existing 0.6 mm wet-net rule. Original project and clearance rules remain intact. Finished-spacing tolerances, return-path noise and populated thermal behavior still need qualification; nominal DRC alone does not close them.

Fifteen evidence checks and four negative controls pass. The controls reject old-width drop, inadequate width margin, a reintroduced narrow arc and half-thickness copper. The current source and all active native boards remain unchanged; this is an integration candidate.

Vocabulary

Rail
A supply-voltage connection distributed to circuit parts.
Quiescent current
Current used by an amplifier with no output load.
Finished-width margin
Minimum manufactured width divided by the calculated required width.
Arc
A curved copper-track section.
Centreline
The geometric path through the middle of a track.
Via barrel
Plated copper inside a hole joining layers.
Clamp injection
Current entering a supply through a protection diode.
DRC
Automated checks against declared board rules.
