# Electric amplifier effects-loop plan

Objective: evaluate and improve the EXISTING J3 EFFECTS SEND / J4 normalled EFFECTS RETURN in the original six-tube Renegade 220 V electric build. User requests research into optical switching, including optocoupler-plus-relay control, and a five-pass external review of the effects-loop addition. This is engineering planning, not a fabrication release. Preserve the received yellow cabinet, functional blocks, constant dry spring-reverb behavior, connectorized insulated wiring and removable rear cover. No extra input buffer or acoustic-build changes.

## Current circuit evidence
Source: engineering/whole-amp/reverb-600/circuit.json, inspected 2026-11-23. K2 selects CLEAN_OUT or DIRTY_OUT onto FX_SEND. J3 tip=FX_SEND; J4 tip=FX_RETURN, normal=FX_SEND, sleeve=GND. C6=47n couples the clean plate into CLEAN_OUT with R43=10M to ground. Dirty source is the 1M master-pot wiper. R100=1M at FX_RETURN is DNP. FX_RETURN drives C601=100n spring send and R612=100k passive dry path to MIX_OUT; C19=22n feeds the phase inverter. Thus an external return currently precedes spring reverb. Existing J3/J4 are passive, not a demonstrated pedal-compatible buffered loop.

K1 selects channel input and grounds the inactive channel; K2 selects channel output. Their coils share COIL_CH. K3 shunts WET_MUTE to ground when de-energized; R610=10k precedes that node. 12V Omron G5V-2-H1 candidates use 150mW reference coils. Q1/Q2 are 2N7000 drivers with local flyback diodes. Reverb opamps are OPA2134PA on +/-15V. Ratings and operating margins need validation; shared GND does not establish galvanic separation.

## Execution
1. Recover interfaces (1–2 hours). Trace both channels, coupling capacitors, J3/J4, spring path, phase inverter, coil/control returns and reset states. Record DC, source impedance and peak signals for clean and dirty settings. Done: pin/net table and level/impedance evidence, unknowns explicitly listed.
2. Specify behavior (1 hour). Target ordinary guitar pedals using separate 6.35mm TS SEND/RETURN jacks. No cables preserves operation; send-only remains usable; return insertion replaces the preamp path. Preserve wet-only expression and reverb mute. Define desired level, loading, bandwidth, bypass policy and location relative to spring reverb before selecting values. Done: state table and acceptance targets.
3. Compare arrangements (2–3 hours). Compare existing passive loop, buffered loop, and buffered loop with bypass. Assess attenuation/recovery, clean-channel loading, noise, clipping, failure behavior, parts and cost. Study ordinary phototransistor optocouplers, LED/LDR controls, optical MOSFET relays and optocoupler-driven mechanical relays separately for K1/K2/K3 and optional loop bypass. Done: source-supported tradeoff and explicit recommended topology without assuming a generic optocoupler replaces a relay.
4. Select and verify (3–6 hours). Use manufacturer documents and China low-quantity supply evidence. Calculate signal scaling, recovery gain, DC protection, supply load and worst-case ratings; simulate loading, frequency response, clipping, switching and power transitions. Keep model limitations explicit. Done: candidate circuit, exact part/package options and repeatable tests, with procurement or physical unknowns retained.
5. Integrate supported design (4–8 hours). Create human-readable schematics, revise BOM and PCB/harness contracts. Local bypass/filter parts, short returns, insulated wires and board connectors are required. Fit two jacks and plug/cable access into the removable chassis; reserve fixed-structure mounts, independent rear cover and power-driver approach. Do not silently add a fourth PCB to the preferred main/power/reverb allocation. Done: native netlist/ERC/DRC and populated fit checks; unresolved physical evidence prevents release.
6. Validate and hand off (3–6 hours plus bench availability). Compare against the existing amp and dry/reverb behavior, test real pedal levels, hum, clipping, pops and startup/shutdown. Log source evidence, calculations, measurements and unresolved items in ICM, rebuild catalogs and prepare reviewed changes for GitHub. Done: bounded evidence report and clear release status, not just clean DRC.

## Risks and resources
Main risks: excessive signal at pedal inputs, clean-channel loading, altered dry/reverb gain from source-impedance changes, switching pops, ground-loop hum, coil noise and jack/cable collisions. Use official component documents, China small-order suppliers, native KiCad, circuit simulation and competent hardware bench measurements. Current full-amp transformer/model issues and actual drill envelope remain unresolved; do not claim measured performance or final mechanical clearance from this plan.
