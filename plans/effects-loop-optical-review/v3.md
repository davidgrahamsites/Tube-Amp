# Electric amplifier effects-loop plan

Objective: evaluate and improve the EXISTING J3 EFFECTS SEND / J4 normalled EFFECTS RETURN in the original six-tube Renegade 220 V electric build. User requests research into optical switching, including optocoupler-plus-relay control, and a five-pass external review of the effects-loop addition. This is engineering planning, not a fabrication release. Preserve the received yellow cabinet, functional blocks, constant dry spring-reverb behavior, connectorized insulated wiring and removable rear cover. No extra input buffer or acoustic-build changes.

## Current circuit evidence
Source: engineering/whole-amp/reverb-600/circuit.json, inspected 2026-11-23. K2 selects CLEAN_OUT or DIRTY_OUT onto FX_SEND. J3 tip=FX_SEND; J4 tip=FX_RETURN, normal=FX_SEND, sleeve=GND. C6=47n couples the clean plate into CLEAN_OUT with R43=10M to ground. Dirty source is the 1M master-pot wiper. R100=1M at FX_RETURN is DNP. FX_RETURN drives C601=100n spring send and R612=100k passive dry path to MIX_OUT; C19=22n feeds the phase inverter. Thus an external return currently precedes spring reverb. Existing J3/J4 are passive, not a demonstrated pedal-compatible buffered loop.

K1 selects channel input and grounds the inactive channel; K2 selects channel output. Their coils share COIL_CH. K3 shunts WET_MUTE to ground when de-energized; R610=10k precedes that node. 12V Omron G5V-2-H1 candidates use 150mW reference coils. Q1/Q2 are 2N7000 drivers with local flyback diodes. Reverb opamps are OPA2134PA on +/-15V. Ratings and operating margins need validation; shared GND does not establish galvanic separation.

## Execution
1. Recover interfaces (1–2 hours). Trace both channels, coupling capacitors, J3/J4, spring path, phase inverter, coil/control returns and reset states. Record DC, source impedance and peak signals for clean and dirty settings. Done: pin/net table and level/impedance evidence, unknowns explicitly listed.
2. Specify behavior (1 hour). Target ordinary guitar pedals using separate 6.35mm TS SEND/RETURN jacks. No cables preserves operation; send-only remains usable; return insertion replaces the preamp path. Preserve wet-only expression and reverb mute. Define desired level, loading, bandwidth, bypass policy and location relative to spring reverb before selecting values. Done: state table and acceptance targets.
3. Compare arrangements (2–3 hours). Compare existing passive loop, buffered loop, and buffered loop with bypass. Assess attenuation/recovery, clean-channel loading, noise, clipping, failure behavior, parts and cost. Study ordinary phototransistor optocouplers, LED/LDR controls, optical MOSFET relays and optocoupler-driven mechanical relays separately for K1/K2/K3 and optional loop bypass. Done: source-supported tradeoff and explicit recommended topology without assuming a generic optocoupler replaces a relay.
4. Select and verify (3–6 hours). Use manufacturer documents and China low-quantity supply evidence. Calculate signal scaling, recovery gain, DC protection, supply load and worst-case ratings; simulate loading, frequency response, clipping, switching and power transitions. Keep model limitations explicit. Done: candidate circuit, exact part/package options and repeatable tests, with procurement or physical unknowns retained.
5. Integrate supported design (4–8 hours). Create human-readable schematics, revise BOM and PCB/harness contracts. Local bypass/filter parts, short returns, insulated wires and board connectors are required. Fit two jacks and plug/cable access into the removable chassis; reserve fixed-structure mounts, independent rear cover and power-driver approach. Do not silently add a fourth PCB to the preferred main/power/reverb allocation. Done: native netlist/ERC/DRC and populated fit checks; unresolved physical evidence prevents release.
6. Validate and hand off (3–6 hours plus bench availability). Compare against the existing amp and dry/reverb behavior, test real pedal levels, hum, clipping, pops and startup/shutdown. Log source evidence, calculations, measurements and unresolved items in ICM, rebuild catalogs and prepare reviewed changes for GitHub. Done: bounded evidence report and clear release status, not just clean DRC.

## Risks and resources
Main risks: excessive signal at pedal inputs, clean-channel loading, altered dry/reverb gain from source-impedance changes, switching pops, ground-loop hum, coil noise and jack/cable collisions. Use official component documents, China small-order suppliers, native KiCad, circuit simulation and competent hardware bench measurements. Current full-amp transformer/model issues and actual drill envelope remain unresolved; do not claim measured performance or final mechanical clearance from this plan.

## Decision gates and qualification contract
Protected: original tube stages, bias and channel behavior; constant dry signal versus reverb knob/pedal; 220V scope; functional block boundaries. Loop buffers, scaling and switching are candidate additions only. This run delivers a reviewed plan and recommendation, not automatic adoption of every researched feature. Native edits follow only a supported design choice. Optical changes beyond the loop require a quantified improvement; otherwise retain current relay functions.

Before detailed component design, screen jack bodies, plug insertion, cable bend radius and connector access against the received cabinet and three-board chassis concept. Check rear cover removal/refitting, driver engagement, hand clearance and servicing without disturbing fixed wiring. Missing actual drill dimensions prevents tool-access signoff, not early envelope studies.

Define numerical design targets for SEND output impedance, allowable pedal input amplitude, RETURN input impedance/headroom, gain, frequency response, added noise and switching transient. Targets must refer to stated loads and frequency bands. No universal 'pedal level' is assumed. Compare channel-to-phase-inverter dry transfer and spring-send drive separately across source impedances, not only overall gain. First qualify an isolated interface fixture; no working assembled amp baseline is assumed. Full-amp unresolved model ringing limits claims.

State table must include no cables, send only, return only, both, half-insertion, unplugging, open/short cables, unpowered pedals, startup, shutdown, brownout and coil/control-supply failure. Establish RETURN DC reference and external offset discharge in each state; R100 DNP is specifically unresolved. Show accessible-port protection from normal tube-node DC, capacitor leakage and relevant coupling faults; electrical safety depends on actual insulation/protection design, not optocoupler isolation labels.

For each optical option, identify switched node, AC/DC voltage, polarity, on resistance, capacitance, leakage, default state, timing, LED/coil demand and lifetime/temperature spread. For optical-plus-relay control draw both power domains and every ground/return path. If the barrier is bridged by shared power/ground, do not claim galvanic isolation or ground-loop removal. Compare against proper existing MOSFET drivers, local coil suppression and controlled current returns. Do not assume relays switch heavy loads: these relays switch audio.

Prototype the loop interface independently before drilling or committing PCB space. Require improvement against a reproducible passive-loop fixture and a written approved target envelope. Engineering-hour estimates above are provisional hands-on effort; supplier response, bench access, physical measurements and redesign lead time are additional and uncertain.

## Source-backed design gates
Retain K1/K2 and their MOSFET coil drivers unless a quantified defect justifies replacement. Do not add optical isolation to shared-power/shared-ground control: it provides no system galvanic separation. Keep a future isolated remote-control option distinct from audio-ground isolation. An optional K3 optical experiment must preserve LED-off mute, satisfy audio voltage and leakage limits, and check capacitance. Panasonic AQY412S Form B has 500pF typical output capacitance; with an ideal10kΩ source this alone gives ~-1.45dB at20kHz. Low-Ron is not enough. No optical candidate is adopted. Manufacturer evidence: https://omronfs.omron.com/en_US/ecb/products/pdf/en-g5v2.pdf ; https://industry.panasonic.com/ap/en/products/control/relay/photomos/number/aqy412s ; https://industry.panasonic.eu/storage/imported/industrial.panasonic.com/ac/cdn/e/control/catalog/semi_eng_selection.pdf . Full sources and dated supplier evidence: evidence/phase-3-research.md.

Determine the actual required return swing BEFORE choosing recovery technology. Historical isolated-preamp simulations (generic models,1MΩ load,not integrated measurements) show ~11.95V peak clean in one ordinary case and much larger clipped extremes. A ±15V OPA2134 cannot be presumed to accept/recreate that range: its common-mode and loaded output limits differ, and typical swing is not a guaranteed bound. Choose low-voltage recovery only if the qualified envelope fits with margin; otherwise evaluate discrete high-voltage recovery at the existing insertion point. An added tube or relocated insertion would change protected architecture and requires an explicit design decision. Sources: https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/opa2134.pdf ; https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/fxlooppcb.html ; https://mojotone.com/products/mojotone-discrete-hi-voltage-series-effects-loop-for-vacuum-tube-amplifiers . These are precedents, not drop-in Renegade qualifications.

R100 DNP leaves a weak indirect ~10.1MΩ DC reference through R612/R24, not a mathematically floating node. Design explicit local references and discharge paths while accounting for their loading. Compare dry-to-PI, spring-send and wet-to-PI transfer separately. Do not replace PI loading by a grounded1MΩ without justification: its grid network is bootstrapped. Lowering RETURN source impedance changes wet/dry balance; a fixed resistor cannot reproduce all dirty-master source impedances.

Provisional fixture targets: SEND≤1kΩ80Hz–10kHz under100kΩ||2nF and1MΩ||2nF; RETURN≥1MΩmidband tested with1kΩ/10kΩ sources. For a DD200 example only, -10dBu nominal and ≤1.23Vpeak SEND provide6dB below its documented+7dBu maximum. No universal pedal compatibility claim. Qualify maximum RETURN separately for every recovery gain and rail corner. Interface unity-equivalent response±0.5dB20Hz–20kHz, THD+N<0.1%1kHz within the qualified envelope; characterize overload separately. Dry and spring transfer change≤0.5dB80Hz–5kHz at reference settings; report all-setting variation and wet transfer rather than hiding it. Normal settled jackDC≤10mV. Quantify noise/transients at explicitly named node/load/bandwidth before signoff; do not call theoretical or model-only checks bench passes. DD200 source: https://www.boss.info/fr/products/dd-200/specifications/ . Targets are proposals, not standards.

Before PCB adoption, compare full passive fail-bypass against buffered bypass. Full bypass must disconnect a failed recovery stage sufficiently to avoid loading the original path; buffered bypass cannot promise operation when its supply fails. Jack-side protection must cover coupling-capacitor leakage/shorts, clamp current and unpowered-rail back-feeding; internal ESD diodes are not complete protection. Source: https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-1387.html .
