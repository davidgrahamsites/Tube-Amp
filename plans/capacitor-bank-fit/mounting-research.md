## Exact-package evidence

**Panasonic EZPV60506MTC.** [Manufacturer R9 specification](https://industrial.panasonic.com/content/data/CP/PDF/ezpv_je.pdf), pages 1–3, 11–14 and 21–24: exposed leads 4±2 mm; maximum body 58×26×41 mm; diameter 1.2±0.2 mm. Avoid excessive lead-root force. Fixing resin needs compatibility/reliability evaluation: solvent, curing heat and shrinkage/expansion can damage the part/leads/joints. No explicit sideways/inverted mounting approval, permitted forming radius/force, clamp pressure or qualified retention hardware was found. Neighbor distance ≥10 mm is recommended against mutual heating. Category −40 to +105°C refers to case surface, including external heat and self-heating. Current and permissible self-rise derate above 70°C; voltage derates above 85°C. The 19.1 A figure applies at 70°C/10 kHz with ΔT<20°C, not arbitrary mains ripple. Applicable current/self-rise curves approach zero at 100°C. Measure the heat-facing surface and maximum surface self-rise using the actual board/current; copper and lead connections affect heat escape. Soldering limits protect the component, not solder-joint manufacturability. These facts do not qualify a bank orientation or allow full ripple at +105°C.

**CRC FC6136.** [Retained manufacturer approval 26124D02](../../engineering/component-selection/evidence/CRC-FC6136.pdf), pages 1–3: exposed leads 4±1 mm; maximum body 58.5×36×51 mm; nominal diameter 1.2 mm without tolerance; 50 µF±10%, 900 VDC. Page 2 gives −40 to +105°C and 25 A maximum current but does not establish the necessary frequency and thermal measurement conditions. Its 100000-hour table entry at hotspot ≤85°C conflicts with the plotted life trend; the existing candidate register already flags this. Do not treat either as guaranteed amplifier life. No mounting orientation, permissible lead forming/root load, body clamp/adhesive specification, neighbor gap or qualified body retention is supplied. Maximum lead diameter, terminal grouping and body-to-pin position remain unresolved. [CRC’s manufacturer DC-link range](https://www.csdcap.com/cate-35403-31887.html) was checked; linked product-detail images returned HTTP 403 through the research tool. No additional exact-part mounting instruction was recovered, and none is inferred from another CRC part.

For context only, [Shinyei’s manufacturer film-capacitor guidance](https://www.shinyei.co.jp/scc/eng/products/use.html) calls for body restraint for large parts or vibration and warns against lead pulling/torsion. This supports investigating a separate mechanical load path; it does **not** qualify Panasonic/CRC hardware, adhesive or orientations. ## Calculation and arrangement implications

For straight through-hole insertion, available underside protrusion is `minimum exposed lead − body seating gap − board thickness`. With the present nominal 1.6 mm board and zero gap, Panasonic gives 0.4 mm and CRC 1.4 mm. A 3 mm lift gives −2.6 mm and −1.6 mm respectively. These are geometric calculations, omitting board tolerance and the required solder protrusion; even seated insertion needs a specified assembly stack. Short leads must not be forced into mismatched holes or used as suspension members. A wider hole calculation is not a released soldering process.

| Arrangement | Evidence-based comparison and remaining condition |
|---|---|
| A: seated dedicated power-PCB area | Baseline with fewest new interfaces. Seated bodies still need an evaluated retention/load path for a vibrating combo. Reserve real neighboring parts, heat, retention and tool access; existing body gaps/holes are not already qualified. |
| B: supported horizontal bank PCB above power PCB | Keep parts seated on their own board; PCB standoffs alone do not restrain bodies. Added height includes lower populated envelope, unresolved electrical/thermal separation, board, body and support. Avoid hot zones and blocked retaining screws. No proof that an upper bank is cooler. |
| C: supported vertical bank PCB beside power PCB | Rotating the complete board can avoid reforming leads but puts body weight/vibration in a different direction relative to joints. Side projection and support thickness replace apparent height savings. Thermal behavior, package orientation and body retention require qualification; neither document gives blanket permission. |

The comparison must distinguish **board orientation** from **bending the capacitor body onto its side relative to the board**. The latter needs a separately approved lead/seat/support arrangement; a CAD rotation is not permission. No new hardware size, clamp force, resin type, airflow benefit or HV clearance is established here. Panasonic’s thermal neighbor recommendation must not become a chassis/fastener/terminal insulation rule, nor be silently transferred to CRC.

## Plan changes and concrete human checks

1. Compare A/B/C with bodies seated and explicit unresolved retention envelopes; exclude elevation on leads alone. Keep A preferred when equivalent. A dimensional shortlist can proceed; final ranking is conditional where support/thermal/insulation dimensions could reverse it.
2. Preserve all four C12/C13/C25/C26 nodes and returns separately within the power block. A daughterboard requires rated removable connections, discharge/protection behavior and reviewed current/return loops; no generic header or merged return is assumed.
3. Qualify exact mounting orientation and support compatibility before adoption: actual four-lead insertion gauge, seating/protrusion check, body restraint/load path, vibration and transport loads. Get exact-part manufacturer guidance where the documents are silent; no supplier contact was performed.
4. Test case temperature at the heat-facing and hottest surfaces with actual ripple, enclosure, board copper, covers and nearby hot parts. Establish CRC current/hotspot/life conditions before using its ratings. Test each proposed arrangement; free-air measurements do not prove assembled thermal behavior.
5. Keep supports wholly on the removable metal power/chassis assembly and fixed cabinet structure. Demonstrate independent wooden rear-cover removal, accessible fasteners, cassette withdrawal and connector service without moving the cover and electronics together.
