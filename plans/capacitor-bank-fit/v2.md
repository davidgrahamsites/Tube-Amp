# Capacitor bank fit improvement

## Objective and boundaries
Choose an economical, serviceable arrangement for C12/C13/C25/C26 in the removable amplifier chassis. Keep the capacitors in the power-supply functional block with unchanged capacitances, nodes and returns. Prefer the intended main audio / power / reverb three-board architecture. A capacitor daughterboard is an alternative with an explicit extra-board cost, not an adopted fourth board. Current native CAD actually contains four incomplete boards; consolidation is unfinished.

The wooden rear cover must remove independently; no electronics attach to it. Preserve tube sockets, functional separation, insulated connectorized harnesses and screw access. No unsupported capacitor bodies on extended leads. Do not alter the cabinet or redesign the audio circuit for packaging.

Known provisional dimensions: cabinet inside width 467 mm, reported depth 160 mm and inside height 383 mm; these are not yet one verified usable rectangular volume. Control opening 307–308 by approximately 53 mm with rear cover on. Chuck diameter 46 mm; bit projection and full drill envelope deferred until tomorrow. Speaker/magnet, transformer and support locations remain unresolved. Current board-only carrier trials exceed available width by 41 mm and depth by 20 mm, so existing layouts are not a proven starting fit.

## Acceptance basis
A local bank fit is not a whole-amplifier fit. The study includes power-board/carrier rearrangement needed to remove the existing width/depth overruns, with Main/Reverb/Control functions preserved. If this requires changes beyond power packaging and already-planned board consolidation, keep the alternative conditional and explain the conflict before adoption. Quantify arrangement A as the baseline; report residual fit deficits for all arrangements.

Map C12/C13/C25/C26 individually to the current circuit's 50 uF requirement, candidate package, supply and return nodes, quantity and required voltage/ripple/thermal conditions. Identical package values do not authorize combining their electrical interfaces. Freeze this reference map before any native edit.

Insulation constraints must come from working and transient voltages, accessible metal/protective-earth relationships, environment and applicable design requirements. Include exposed terminals, underside copper, fasteners, brackets, connectors and insulating supports. Unresolved insulation values are blockers, not guessed numeric clearances. Manufacturer body-spacing recommendations are not HV insulation distances.

## Steps
1. Freeze evidence and frames (integration, one focused session). Record native board hashes and read the mechanical fit register. Classify dimensions as measured, manufacturer, assumption or unknown. Establish cabinet, board, cover and component datums. Classify each unknown as blocking final ranking, blocking fabrication only, or bounded for a preliminary comparison; do not turn unknown speaker/magnetics space into free volume. Done: one sourced input table and explicit list of missing transforms; no image-derived dimensions.
2. Build exact candidate envelopes (component/mechanical owner, one session). Panasonic EZPV60506MTC maximum body 58 × 26 × 41 mm, CRC FC6136 58.5 × 36 × 51 mm. Both 50 uF candidates remain unselected; voltage/ripple/hot-life and insertion checks remain required. Map all four references first. Add support, terminal, board-edge, connector and service envelopes. Obtain package-specific mounting orientation, body-retention and lead-strain evidence; board standoffs alone do not restrain capacitor bodies. Do not infer electrical clearance from a geometric gap. Done: reproducible body/lead calculations and explicit unqualified dimensions.
3. Compare three arrangements (mechanical owner, one to two sessions): A dedicated area on repacked power PCB; B supported horizontal capacitor board above an appropriate cool power zone; C supported vertical capacitor board beside the power PCB. Rotate/group bodies only where the exact package permits. All supports attach to the metal cassette, not the removable wood or electrical headers. Include existing power components, thermal regions, speaker and magnetics whenever dimensions exist. Done: common-scale candidate views and comparison of total assembly volume, part count, wiring and service sequence; missing dimensions remain conditional.
4. Check functional and service interfaces (electrical/mechanical owners, one session). Preserve each capacitor node/return and short current loops; qualify connector voltages, current and insulation. Account for discharge/protection, thermal conditions, vibration support, tube extraction, harness bend radius and fastener tool sweeps. Keep hot resistors/regulators and sensitive audio out from under a bank. Prefer straight retaining-screw access; flexible screwdriver adapters are optional aids, not a waiver. Distinguish drilling/deburring bare metal before electronics installation from driving fasteners at each assembly stage. Test a common sequence: rear cover off/on without disconnecting wiring or releasing electronics; retaining screws; cassette withdrawal; bank/PCB replacement on bench; reverse reassembly. A full-size inert mock-up may check handling and extraction before detailed manufacture. Done: pass/fail/unknown matrix and service sequence with evidence for each candidate, with electrical/thermal/vibration tests still open.
5. Select and verify (integration, one to two sessions plus later physical check). Rank feasible arrangements by simplicity, usable fit, cooling, access, cost and noise risk; prefer A if equivalent. Generate an editable parametric FreeCAD study and a dimensioned review from the same input parameters. Check exported solid bounds/interferences and manually inspect views. Adopt native placement/footprints only after the selected exact parts and interfaces are verified; rerun ERC/DRC/net preservation and populated fit for any native change. Done: a justified preferred candidate or an explicit conditional shortlist, with next measurements and manufacturing holds.
6. Preserve and hand off (integration and Luna journal, one short session). Save evidence and decisions in ICM, update the design journal, run scoped ICM and input/output checks, rebuild catalogs, commit and push only scoped work under the existing user authorization for this amplifier project; no supplier disclosure or manufacturing upload. Done: reproducible files and Git receipt, no unsupported fit or fabrication claim.

## Risks and resources
Use manufacturer drawings, local KiCad and FreeCAD, exact purchased-part evidence when available, and the user's cabinet/tool measurements. Principal risks are short leads, lead-root stress, underside high voltage, blocked cooling or screws, longer noisy charging loops, unselected tall parts and a trapped withdrawal path. Physical thermal/vibration and assembly tests remain required; a CAD screen cannot establish them. The decision should minimize additional boards and custom hardware while respecting every hard constraint.
