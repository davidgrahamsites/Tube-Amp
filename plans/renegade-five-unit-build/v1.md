# Renegade five-unit amplifier project

## Objective and starting assumptions
Create an editable KiCad 10 amplifier design for 220 V / 50 Hz use in China, adding tube-driven spring reverb and debounced clean/dirty and reverb footswitch control. Deliver a sleek front panel, cabinet graphics, a documented BOM and supplier shortlist, drilled PCB manufacturing files, a tested prototype and an economical five-unit build.

Current inputs are the recovered chat, handoff and three-symbol library starter. The original amplifier PPTX is missing. Power, original tube complement and dimensions are not yet established. Preserve the source circuit before modifying it.

Confirmed: combo amplifier with a built-in speaker; source and deliver in Shanghai, China. The user has some build experience and substantial test-equipment experience; preserve explicit construction and bench guidelines. Working assumptions: five finished units total, one prototype plus four copies, manual assembly; power and budget remain open.

## Brainstorm and preferred starting direction
- Software and automation: compare free KiCad, parametric mechanical CAD, vector graphics, circuit simulation and scripting tools. Create a discoverable Circuit Design Skill Ladder that routes tasks by evidence, tool capability and review needs. Prefer parameter-driven models, native CLIs and reproducible exports; use GUI control only where required.
- Construction: compare a single large PCB, modular audio/power/control boards, and a hybrid with chassis-mounted tubes, transformers, jacks and pots. Favor the hybrid as a prototype candidate; choose after wiring, noise, heat and total cost review.
- Cabinet: compare a standard metal chassis in a plywood combo cabinet, a ready-made combo shell, and fabricated chassis plus locally cut wood. Account for speaker load, baffle strength, ventilation, tank orientation/isolation, handle loads and service access. Avoid tooling-dependent molded parts.
- Appearance: explore matte black with ivory labels and one accent, brushed metal with black labels, and an off-white faceplate with restrained graphics. Align knobs, group channel controls, make labels readable and preserve access to switches and jacks.
- Graphics: compare printed adhesive overlays, direct UV printing and screen printing. Prepare editable vector artwork, separate print and cut/drill layers, and a physical scale proof. Obtain permission for any borrowed logo/art.
- Cost: compare domestic low-MOQ bare-board vendors, hand assembly versus assembly service, ready-made versus custom cabinet, and separate shipping versus consolidation.
- Reverb and controls: preserve the parallel dry path, inspect the effects-loop/phase-inverter interfaces, verify tank/transformer/driver compatibility, and decide reverb tails, manual controls and pedal behavior.

## Reviews, resources and sequencing
Review the source baseline before additions; approve electrical/mechanical interfaces before layout; inspect fabrication outputs before ordering; validate the prototype before committing to four copies. Early pricing and visual exploration can proceed while the source is missing. PCB routing cannot start responsibly without selected packages and voltage/current/mechanical constraints.

Resources: installed KiCad tools; original source; manufacturer documentation; supplier quotes; free mechanical/graphics CAD and scripting tools; a builder and qualified tube-amplifier technician; suitable test equipment and a speaker load. Record the required equipment and procedures before energizing. CAD/ERC/DRC results do not constitute electrical safety approval.

Elapsed schedule depends on source availability, review and supplier lead times; task effort is active work, not calendar time. Track the critical path from source to reviewed circuitry, physical layout, prototype hardware, validation and batch release.

## Five-unit cost model
Prepare low/base/high RMB estimates for transformers/choke, tubes/sockets, reverb, boards, passives/control parts, speaker, baffle, chassis, cabinet, knobs/jacks, front panel/graphics, pedal, wiring, shipping/tax, tools/test access, labor and rework. No supplier prices have been verified in this draft.

For five accepted units, total = shared design/tool/test costs + one prototype's parts and labor + four repeat kits and labor + unavoidable MOQ extras + shipping/tax + contingency. Report total, effective cost per accepted unit, marginal repeat cost, cash spend and imputed labor separately. Show both five-unit and alternative prototype-plus-five scenarios if scope changes.

Use measured board area, actual selected BOM and cabinet drawings for quotes. Minimize total delivered cost subject to verified electrical and mechanical suitability; a cheap transformer with unknown ratings is ineligible. Keep premium cosmetic options outside the base build.

## Risks and deliverables
Largest risks: missing original source; incompatible transformers/tank/speaker; unreviewed mains/high-voltage construction; hum, oscillation and switching pops; footprint/mechanical mismatch; cabinet/tank fit; misleading low-MOQ pricing; five units ordered before prototype fixes.

Deliver editable KiCad project and portable libraries, source map, calculations, revision-linked BOM, supplier evidence/quotes, mechanical drawings, vector graphics, drilled-board fabrication/assembly packages, prototype and per-unit test records, five-unit cost report, and updated engineering brain. This plan authorizes planning and reversible design work; purchase and production commitments require a concrete quote and spending decision.
