Process: read-only circuit/record inspection, primary manufacturer guidance and conditional arithmetic. Output: allocation consequences and qualification gates, not a selected harness or circuit change. Heater drop and reference uncertainty

The existing source-bound nominal heater inventory is 4 × 0.3 A ECC83S + 2 × 1.5 A EL34 = 4.20 A at 6.3 V. Existing midpoint resistors add 6.3/200 = 0.0315 A; the unselected pilot allowance adds 0.150 A: 4.3815 A. These values are nominal/allowance only, not a maximum, cold-start surge or winding rating. JJ ECC83S, JJ EL34/E34L, local load inventory and limitations.

Conditional resistive calculation, with total loop resistance including both conductors, all contacts and terminations:

Vdrop = I × Rloop; Ploop = I² × Rloop.

At the existing 4.3815 A inventory, each 10 mΩ of loop resistance costs 43.815 mV and 0.192 W. If an engineering budget of 0.10 V were later justified, it would permit only 22.82 mΩ total loop resistance at this inventory; 0.20 V would permit 45.65 mΩ. These are arithmetic sensitivities, not adopted allowable drops. The loaded transformer voltage at each mains corner, actual tube heater tolerance, conductor temperature, startup current and socket/contact drop remain inputs. Model the AC loop impedance where necessary and measure true RMS across the most distant socket. Measure cold start and hot operation in both mains modes; do not infer socket voltage from transformer terminals.

A wire-size calculation can follow once length and construction are known: Rloop(T) = 2 × length × resistance_per_length(T) + sum(contact/termination resistances), adjusted for branches rather than pretending every socket draws the full group current. Use the selected wire maker's maximum resistance and actual contact specification. Do not choose gauge merely from ampacity; heater voltage drop may dominate before ampacity does.

The latest HD-002 is a blocking interface result: shared HREF has no permissible interval in 14 of 38 declared model cases; its nominal best-case DC reference resistance exceeds the reference JJ EL34 limit. Separate isolated heater groups are a candidate, not an adopted fix. Four preamp heaters nominally require 1.2 A and the two EL34s 3.0 A, before separate midpoint/pilot/startup budgets. At the same 10 mΩ loop resistance these isolated groups would lose 12 mV/30 mV and 0.0144 W/0.090 W respectively. A center tap on one continuous winding does not create two galvanically independent supplies.

Do not freeze a two-wire heater connector, adopt extra windings, or claim an isolated split solves everything: the highest modeled preamp case still fails its shared preamp reference window. Both triodes in V3 share a heater. The exact tubes and complete reference/fault envelope must be resolved first. Elevated heaters also require common-mode insulation to other harness circuits and chassis; their 6.3 V differential is not the whole insulation stress. Preserve close paired heater routing, its insulation and separation from sensitive grids; twisting does not repair insufficient voltage/current rating.

Local bypass and lead parasitics

Remote bulk storage must not absorb existing local bypass components into the bottom box simply because all are called capacitors. TI's exact OPA2134 guidance places ceramic bypasses at each supply pin and ground with short connections; its current datasheet recommends 10 nF. That value is source guidance, not authorization to replace the native capacitor values. TI OPAx134 SBOS058B, sections 7.3–7.4. Preserve the existing local control/reverb bypass and relay-clamp work, with its latest native checks; requalify regulator sockets, input/output capacitors and protection against the chosen physical allocation.

For B and C, derive or measure cable R/L and coupling using the actual route, then compare rail impedance and load transients with the same load model and capacitor ESR/ESL. ΔV = R × ΔI + L × di/dt is a useful local sensitivity; f0 = 1/(2π√(LC)) identifies an idealized resonance to investigate, not an actual predicted oscillation without source/load resistance and distributed wiring. No cable inductance value has been assumed here. Include open return, one-rail disconnect, standby transition and regulator reverse-current paths. Recheck the existing reset/mute behavior using rail voltages at the load, because cable drop/collapse can differ from the source measurement.

Draw recharge and load-return loops separately. Run a supplied rail with its intentional return route rather than returning it through unrelated audio wiring. Multiple same-net return conductors are not automatically correct either: inventory the shared paths, including signal interconnects, tank shields and chassis bonds, so current division is explicit. PE or the enclosure must never be the normal return under the project contract.

PE continuity and stored charge

The grounding contract already requires J7.3 directly to a dedicated safety stud with no PCB, switch, fuse or signal harness in series. Every accessible conductive enclosure, upper chassis and decorative panel needs its own verified bond in the assembly schedule. GB1 remains the separate permanent GND/chassis bond near the quiet input; a second metal box does not authorize another GND/chassis bond. Independent rear-cover removal must not remove protective bonding. If service requires disconnecting a protective bond, the written reassembly procedure must restore and verify it before any energized test. Physical conductors, hardware, surface preparation, continuity/fault tests and applicable acceptance limits remain unqualified.

Do not assume the source side alone can be charged. The existing capacitors create independent energy stores when a cable is removed:

Useful conditional arithmetic: C29/C30 are two nominal 220 µF capacitors in series, hence nominal effective capacitance 110 µF under equal voltage sharing. Their retained 220 kΩ bleeder per capacitor gives an isolated nominal time constant of 48.4 s. This is not the actual full-network discharge time or a guaranteed service delay; tolerance, faults, unequal sharing and other connected stores matter. Stored energy is ½CV²: one 50 µF capacitor at a hypothetical 400 V stores 4 J; the nominal series raw pair at a hypothetical total 400 V stores 8.8 J. These voltages are illustrative, not measured operating values. Each actual rail needs its own maximum voltage/tolerance calculation and test points.

Before accepting a removable assembly, electrical integration must specify an ordered power-isolation, discharge, voltage-verification, unplug/replug and PE-recheck procedure, with justified residual-voltage limits and retained paths on each side. Do not invent a waiting time or add an unreviewed bleeder as a packaging shortcut. Candidate capacitor charge/discharge restrictions still apply; see the previous manufacturer-backed capacitor review.

120/220 V controls placement

The preserved mains decision requires 120 V/60 Hz and 220 V/50 Hz, voltage-specific protection and a recessed marked selector operated only with power disconnected. The native baseline still has 220 V T1 and T3 references; it is not a dual-voltage implementation. Both mains-powered transformers must be handled by the selected arrangement, not only T1. No final fuse values follow from this packaging research.

For the lower-enclosure comparison, group inlet, protective stud, voltage selection, fuses and transformer primaries below. Locate the actual power switch there if acceptable access can be shown. If the user's upper control location is retained, explicitly count the long mains loop and its separate guarded routing/strain relief. Likewise an upper S2 sends B_RAW/B_MAIN between modules; keeping its HV switching function below may require a user-interface change or a separately reviewed remote actuation design. These costs must not disappear from the comparison.

Schurter documents selector inserts for different winding arrangements and approval only together with their matching power-entry module. Its table includes 115/230 and 120/240 two-winding markings and other tapped arrangements; none establishes this project's 120/220 supply or selects a part. Schurter selector architecture. Schurter's IEC appliance-coupler guidance is for AC mains couplers with defined voltage/current/temperature conditions; it is not evidence that a familiar mains-style connector is suitable for the separate HVDC rails. Vocabulary key

Terms used in this report. See the full key for related terms.

AC
Alternating current; current that changes direction.
B_MAIN
The supply node that feeds the output-transformer centre tap.
B_RAW
The rectified supply node before the standby switch.
B_SCREEN
The filtered supply node for the output-tube screen grids.
B_PRE
The filtered supply node for the specified preamplifier stages.
B_EARLY
The filtered supply node for the earlier preamplifier stages.
Bias
A steady voltage or current that sets a device operating point.
Bleeder
A resistor used to remove capacitor charge; its presence alone does not prove safe residual voltage.
Capacitance
The ratio of stored charge to voltage; measured in farads (F).
Capacitor
A component that stores charge and electrical energy.
Chassis
The metal structure that supports and encloses the electronics.
Clearance
The shortest distance through air between conductive parts.
CT
Centre tap; an electrical connection part-way along a transformer winding.
DC
Direct current; current that flows in one direction.
Derating
Operating below a component rating to allow for heat or other specified conditions.
Envelope
The space occupied by a part or assembly, including stated allowances.
Feedback
A signal returned from an output to an earlier stage to control gain or response.
GB1
The design identifier for the declared signal-ground to chassis connection.
Ground
A named circuit reference; it is not automatically protective earth.
Harness
A grouped set of wires, contacts and connectors.
Heater
The tube element that heats the cathode.
HREF
The design name for the heater reference node.
HV
High voltage; use the actual voltage and insulation requirement in each design check.
Impedance
Opposition to alternating current, including resistance and frequency-dependent effects; measured in ohms (Ω).
Lead
A component terminal that connects to the board.
Load
The circuit or device that takes current from a source.
Loop resistance
The total resistance of the supply and return conductors, contacts and terminations.
Mains
The building AC power supply.
Net
A named set of electrically connected points.
Node
An electrical connection shared by specified components.
Nominal
The stated reference value before tolerance and operating variation.
PCB
Printed circuit board.
PE
Protective earth; the protective connection to exposed conductive parts.
Preamp
Preamplifier; the stages that prepare the input signal for the power amplifier.
Qualification
Checks that show a design meets stated limits under stated conditions.
Rectifier
A circuit that changes alternating current into current of one polarity.
Reservoir
A capacitor bank that stores energy after rectification.
Return path
The route that carries current back to its source.
Ripple
Periodic supply variation remaining after rectification and filtering.
Schematic
A drawing of electrical connections and component functions.
Series
Connected in sequence so the same branch current passes through each part.
Socket
A replaceable connection that holds a tube or integrated circuit.
Standby
A switch state; it does not by itself prove the amplifier is de-energized.
Strain relief
A support that keeps cable forces away from electrical terminations.
Tank
The mechanical spring-reverb assembly.
Tolerance
The permitted variation from a specified value.
Transformer
A magnetic device that transfers energy between windings.
Winding
A coil of insulated wire in a transformer or inductor.
Stored energy
Energy held in a charged capacitor; it depends on capacitance and voltage.
