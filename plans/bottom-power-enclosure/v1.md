# Bottom-mounted power enclosure study

## Objective
Evaluate the user's idea of a metal power enclosure mounted to the fixed cabinet floor, with insulated connectorized cabling to the upper tube/audio assembly. Preserve the approximately 25 W design, switchable 120/220 V requirement, socketed tubes/chips, functional blocks and independent rear cover. Favor reliable economical China-source parts. This is a proposal: earlier records place power above and the spring tank on the floor; no bottom enclosure is already dimensioned or approved.

## Plan
1. Freeze a common cabinet model and ownership map (integration, one session). Record speaker basket/magnet, received tank 149.5×60×36.5 mm plus plugs/mounts, floor supports, upper assembly and withdrawal paths. Cabinet 467/160/383 mm readings lack common verified datums. Done: sourced occupied/unknown volumes and baseline deficits, with no invented free space.
2. Compare three distributions (electrical/mechanical, one session): A previous upper-power concept; B lower mains/auxiliary transformer, rectification and bulk reservoir with stage filtering/local bypass above; C lower power assembly including suitable filter capacitors, with local decoupling retained where required. C12=B_EARLY/GND, C13+C25=B_PRE/GND distinct50u refs, C26=B_SCREEN/GND; C29/C30 raw reservoir. Keep values and nodes unchanged. Evaluate choke placement and output transformer separately; do not move the output transformer downstairs merely because it is a transformer. Done: component allocation and circuit-boundary table for every option.
3. Research and specify harness/metalwork interfaces (electrical/mechanical, one to two sessions). Bound real conductor lengths, DC/ripple/fault current, heater voltage drop, supply/return loops, connector ratings and unmated protection. Define permanent PE bonding for accessible metal, controlled signal/chassis bond, strain relief, grommets and wrong-mate prevention. No chassis as normal return. Evaluate moving whole power PCB versus splitting only physical placement while keeping function intact. Done: pin-level candidate interface and explicit unresolved voltage/current/insulation gates, not an assumed connector family.
4. Compare whole-system performance (integration, one to two sessions). Assess tank/input magnetic pickup, transformer orientation/separation, mains/rectifier and heater harness routing, output/feedback loop stability, enclosed heat, heavy-component floor loads, speaker travel and ventilation, and both assemblies' retention/removal. A metal box is not assumed to eliminate low-frequency magnetic hum. Distinguish mains isolation/shielding from signal return design. Done: hard pass/fail/unknown matrix; no weighted score hides a failure.
5. Prove service and qualify a preferred candidate (integration and builder, one to two sessions plus physical time). Preserve local supply/bypass behavior and compare matched noise/load/thermal/feedback models. Make inert full-size fit mock-up, then prototype with applicable gates passed and actual authorization. Verify cover off/on independently, upper/lower module removal, tool sweeps and harness slack/disconnection, charged/off/standby/partly unplugged states and retained discharge paths. Full drill data remains pending tomorrow;46mm chuck alone is insufficient. Done: justified preferred distribution or conditional shortlist with remaining owners; native changes only after interface review and renewed ERC/DRC/net/fit checks, final production after physical qualification.
6. Save handoff (integration/Luna, short session). Log sources and decisions in ICM, update journal/catalogs, scoped checks and authorized Git push. Done: distinguish research completion from fit/release completion.

## Decision rule and risks
Hard requirements first, then compare net usable volume, hum risk, wiring burden, service, cost and weight. Keeping local filtering near loads may be better than moving every capacitor downstairs. Bottom mounting competes with tank and speaker, needs a load path into fixed cabinet structure, and may exchange crowded PCB space for long high-voltage/high-current cabling. Exact transformer, speaker, tank hum response, cable routes, insulation and actual tool geometry remain unresolved. No capacitor, transformer, connector or enclosure is adopted by this plan.
