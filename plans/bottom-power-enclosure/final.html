Bottom-mounted power enclosure study

Objective

Evaluate the user's idea of a metal power enclosure mounted to the fixed cabinet floor, with insulated connectorized cabling to the upper tube/audio assembly. Preserve the approximately 25 W design, switchable 120/220 V requirement, socketed tubes/chips, functional blocks and independent rear cover. Favor reliable economical China-source parts. This is a proposal: earlier records place power above and the spring tank on the floor; no bottom enclosure is already dimensioned or approved.

Acceptance and scope

Freeze a preservation table for mains inlet/selection/fuses/switching, each supply rail and its returns, heaters/reference groups, auxiliary/control/reverb, output transformer/speaker/feedback, tube and chip socket service. Both120V/60Hz and220V/50Hz cases must be checked. Derive socket-heater limits from the exact tube ratings, output/headroom and noise limits from current product requirements/baseline, capacitor temperatures from actual derated ratings, and dimensional clearances from justified mechanical/electrical inputs. Record metric, method, limit and authority before any pass; unresolved limits mean unknown, not invented compliance.

Bound this study to physical allocation and interface consequences. Screen whole-Power-PCB relocation first where possible; a split implementation requires a separately estimated change list. If any candidate needs new regulation, added capacitance, changed switching or protection, record it as an electrical redesign dependency instead of silently incorporating it. Use one preliminary allocation/interface revision as a checkpoint, not a false feasibility deadline. If more iteration is justified, document a bounded next question and effort before proceeding; unresolved external evidence cannot be repaired by arbitrary repeated geometry changes.

Decision rule and risks

Hard requirements first, then compare net usable volume, hum risk, wiring burden, service, cost and weight. Keeping local filtering near loads may be better than moving every capacitor downstairs. Bottom mounting competes with tank and speaker, needs a load path into fixed cabinet structure, and may exchange crowded PCB space for long high-voltage/high-current cabling. Exact transformer, speaker, tank hum response, cable routes, insulation and actual tool geometry remain unresolved. No capacitor, transformer, connector or enclosure is adopted by this plan.

Research integration

  • Magnetic research: retain T2 output transformer near upper output tubes as first baseline; compare L1 separately. Enclosure material receives no assumed50/60Hz attenuation. Include direct magnetic pickup, conducted noise and mechanical buzz as distinct paths. Preserve tank horizontal/open-side-down; no uncosted relocation or use of power lid as tank mount. Use closed technician-reviewed loaded fixtures and power-off repositioning, matched recovery load/gain, repeat baseline, both mains modes, and declared absolute noise limits/measurement uncertainty.
  • Harness research: B specifically places L1/C26 below, R35 with C13/C25 above and R17 with C12 above; preserve distinct references. B_MAIN must also reach upper T2 CT. Both B and C need bias/auxiliary ownership. Bias-contact or return loss while B+ remains is a blocking fault condition. HD-002 prevents freezing heater winding/group/reference or final pin count. At the existing nominal-plus-pilot4.3815A inventory,10mΩ full loop loses43.815mV; not a wire-size or accepted drop. Include startup and loaded socket voltage, regulator local input/output caps and load-end reset/mute timing.
  • Existing S1 carries mains and S2 carries B_RAW/B_MAIN. Keeping them upper means real long mains/HV loops; placing switching below changes user access. Remote actuation is a separately assessed circuit change. Do not silently move controls or add a relay.
  • Both module halves may retain charge; PE bonds remain independent of signal harness and GB1 remains one deliberate signal/chassis bond. Define discharge/test-point ownership for raw, film, bias and auxiliary stores, including partially unplugged/open-return states. AC mains connector appearance/rating does not qualify HVDC connectors.
  • Mechanical research: floor construction and full dimensions unknown. Whole160×175mm Power board is not proven to fit a160mm reported depth before enclosure/mounts. Structural load path runs via metal to fixed cabinet; no transformer mass on PCB supports or rear cover. Account for lower-lid and both module extraction paths, speaker, tank plugs/bag, wiring and actual tool sweeps. Thermal review includes upward heating of the upper assembly.
  • Existing China magnetic leads are not exact electrical/physical selections; no credible lower-module price delta yet. Keep dated quantity-tier prices distinct from single-unit delivered quotes and do not add overlapping old budget categories.

Preference for the next comparison: B, hybrid lower bulk/screen power and upper stage filtering, against A. C is conditional because longer filtered-node loops may erase packaging benefits. This is a conditional research preference, not architecture adoption.

Pre-mortem acceptance order

Reject or hold B/C before detailed fabrication if floor/tank/speaker occupancy cannot be resolved, hum cannot meet its declared threshold in a feasible location, upper S1/S2 routing defeats the benefit, bias/contact faults remain unacceptable, the lower lid/module is trapped, or reinforcement/thermal/harness costs erase the gain. A passing static box screen cannot advance through these failures. Carry control-location and any protection redesign as explicit user-visible decisions after concrete alternatives are prepared. Prototype drawings are distinct from production release; no supplier order is authorized by this review.

Vocabulary key

Terms used in this report. See the full key for related terms.

AC
Alternating current; current that changes direction.
B_MAIN
The supply node that feeds the output-transformer centre tap.
B_RAW
The rectified supply node before the standby switch.
B_SCREEN
The filtered supply node for the output-tube screen grids.
B_PRE
The filtered supply node for the specified preamplifier stages.
B_EARLY
The filtered supply node for the earlier preamplifier stages.
Bias
A steady voltage or current that sets a device operating point.
Capacitance
The ratio of stored charge to voltage; measured in farads (F).
Capacitor
A component that stores charge and electrical energy.
Chassis
The metal structure that supports and encloses the electronics.
Chuck
The drill part that grips the bit.
CT
Centre tap; an electrical connection part-way along a transformer winding.
DC
Direct current; current that flows in one direction.
DRC
Design Rule Check; a PCB geometry and connectivity check.
ERC
Electrical Rules Check; a schematic consistency check.
Feedback
A signal returned from an output to an earlier stage to control gain or response.
GB1
The design identifier for the declared signal-ground to chassis connection.
Harness
A grouped set of wires, contacts and connectors.
Headroom
The signal margin before clipping or another specified limit.
Heater
The tube element that heats the cathode.
HV
High voltage; use the actual voltage and insulation requirement in each design check.
ICM
Interpretable Context Methodology; the project structure that records ownership, inputs, outputs and checks.
Load
The circuit or device that takes current from a source.
Magnetics
Transformers and inductors.
Mains
The building AC power supply.
Net
A named set of electrically connected points.
Node
An electrical connection shared by specified components.
Nominal
The stated reference value before tolerance and operating variation.
PCB
Printed circuit board.
PE
Protective earth; the protective connection to exposed conductive parts.
Qualification
Checks that show a design meets stated limits under stated conditions.
Rectifier
A circuit that changes alternating current into current of one polarity.
Reservoir
A capacitor bank that stores energy after rectification.
Ripple
Periodic supply variation remaining after rectification and filtering.
Socket
A replaceable connection that holds a tube or integrated circuit.
Standby
A switch state; it does not by itself prove the amplifier is de-energized.
Strain relief
A support that keeps cable forces away from electrical terminations.
Tank
The mechanical spring-reverb assembly.
Transformer
A magnetic device that transfers energy between windings.
Winding
A coil of insulated wire in a transformer or inductor.
Prototype
A test assembly used to check the design before release.
