Renegade amplifier · Bench planning · 26 November 2026

Test equipment and requirements

Start with a low-voltage protection fixture. The complete amplifier is not needed for this test. Select equipment in stages; borrow specialist safety equipment or use a qualified workshop where practical.

Scope: approximately 25 W tube amplifier, selectable 120 V / 220 V, spring reverb and removable assemblies. This guide specifies equipment capability and test evidence; it is not an approved energizing procedure or a purchasing list of qualified models.

1. First test: protection startup and reset

The present simulation models do not establish reliable power-up behaviour. Build a small fixture containing the proposed comparator, precision reference, shunt regulator, optocouplers, resistors and actual socket adapters. Exact parts and the fixture drawing must be fixed before assembly.

  • Use a current-limited low-voltage DC supply and an adjustable \(0\text{–}3\,\mathrm V\) sense signal with approximately \(10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) source resistance.
  • Keep mains wiring, tube supplies and high-voltage pass devices disconnected.
  • Measure the supply, reference, comparator outputs, threshold input and optocoupler LED current during startup, power loss and sense sweeps.
  • Check fast and slow supply changes, interrupted power, trip, release and repeated switching; repeat at the agreed temperature limits.

The fixture must reproduce the declared monitor-current range. The conservative valid-rail threshold is \(5\,\mathrm V\); this is a measurement boundary, not a complete supply specification. The test plan must define ramp rates, current limits, timing limits and acceptance criteria before a result is credited.

This test qualifies the low-voltage control circuit only. It does not prove high-voltage insulation, feed-resistor heating, full-supply overshoot or safe fault interruption. Source: HD-012 reset and startup review.

4. Measurement calculations

For voltage across a known sense resistor:

\[I_{\mathrm{rms}}=\frac{V_{R,\mathrm{rms}}}{R_s}\]

For a resistive output load:

\[P=\frac{V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2}{R_L},\qquad V_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{P R_L}\]

Example only: \(25\,\mathrm W\) into \(8\,\Omega\) requires approximately \(14.14\,\mathrm{V_{rms}}\) and \(1.77\,\mathrm{A_{rms}}\). This does not select an 8 Ω tap or certify 25 W output; the actual load and distortion limit remain part of the test definition.

Impedance requires phase as well as amplitude:

\[Z(f)=R_s\frac{\underline V_{\mathrm{tank}}(f)}{\underline V_R(f)}\]

Underlined voltages are complex quantities: amplitude and phase. Correct for fixture and instrument loading. DC resistance is not AC impedance, and impedance does not establish a safe maximum drive current.

5. Save one record for each test

  • Test ID, date, operator, circuit revision, exact parts and assembly photographs.
  • Instrument model, probe settings, calibration check and connection diagram.
  • Mains mode where applicable, temperature, load, signal and current limit.
  • Acceptance limits fixed before testing; raw waveforms and measurements with units.
  • Pass/fail, uncertainty, defect, correction and retest reference.

High-voltage boundary: use a competent technician for energized mains/tube-supply and safety tests. Keep protective earth intact; never lift the scope earth to obtain a measurement. Never connect a high-voltage node or unattenuated speaker output to the recording interface.

Vocabulary key

Fixture
A small test assembly with defined connections and loads.
Comparator
A device that switches its output when one input voltage crosses another.
Reference
A stable voltage used as a measurement or switching standard.
Shunt regulator
A device that controls voltage by drawing current from a supplied node.
Optocoupler
A device that transfers a signal through light across an electrical isolation barrier.
DC / AC
Direct current / alternating current.
Oscilloscope
An instrument that records voltage against time.
Impedance
Frequency-dependent opposition to alternating current, including phase.
LCR meter
An instrument for inductance, capacitance and resistance measurements at specified test conditions.
Dummy load
A resistor assembly that absorbs amplifier output power in place of the speaker.
RMS
Root mean square; the effective value used for resistive power calculations.
Attenuator
A circuit that reduces signal level.
Differential probe
A probe that measures the voltage between two points within specified voltage-to-earth limits.
Bias
A steady electrical condition that sets a device's operating point.
Headroom
The signal margin before clipping or another operating limit.
Clipping / distortion
Signal flattening at a limit / unwanted alteration of the waveform.
Protective bond
The low-resistance safety connection between exposed metal and protective earth.
Withstand test
A specified insulation test using elevated voltage and controlled limits.
Harness
Wires, contacts and connectors assembled as a unit.
Carrier / socket adapter
A removable support that connects a component to a compatible socket.
