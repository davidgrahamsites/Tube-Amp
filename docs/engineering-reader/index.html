What prevents release

  • The present carrier studies exceed the provisional cabinet frame by 41 mm in width or 20 mm in depth. These are different arrangements, not a single measured assembly.
  • The native heater reference still fails; a separate-group correction passes conditional normal bounds but needs a real supply ceiling and fault protection.
  • Exact transformers, speaker, capacitor assemblies and connectors remain unresolved.
  • The current native CAD has four incomplete boards; the intended build has three.
  • The 46 mm drill chuck is known; bit reach and motor-body clearance are pending.

These findings require corrections. They do not prove that a revised assembly cannot fit. Repeated reviews cannot replace those corrections.

Work order

  1. Close the electrical interfaces. Resolve the heater reference and bias-loss cases before fixing transformer windings or connector contacts. Record the circuit change and rerun the affected checks.
  2. Select the large parts. Obtain exact electrical ratings, maximum dimensions, mounting details and China supply evidence. Bound missing dimensions; do not treat them as clearances.
  3. Check one complete assembly. Include the speaker, tank, boards, sockets, cables, supports, covers and fastener tool paths. Use a full-size inert mock-up before fabrication. Record each collision and correction.
  4. Finish the boards and harness. Recheck trace current capacity, insulation, holes, returns, bypass placement and accessible connectors after placement changes.
  5. Verify the populated prototype. Check output, both mains settings, protection, hum, temperature, stability and service access against stated limits before production release.

No further five-pass review starts automatically. The next checkpoint must produce a corrected circuit, exact part evidence or a populated fit result.

Vocabulary key

Terms used in this report. See the full key for related terms.

Bias
A steady voltage or current that sets a device operating point.
Capacitor
A component that stores charge and electrical energy.
Chuck
The drill part that grips the bit.
Clearance
The shortest distance through air between conductive parts.
Harness
A grouped set of wires, contacts and connectors.
Heater
The tube element that heats the cathode.
Heater reference
The voltage used to set the heater circuit relative to the tube cathodes.
Load
The circuit or device that takes current from a source.
Mains
The building AC power supply.
Populated
With the components installed.
Preamp
Preamplifier; the stages that prepare the input signal for the power amplifier.
Reservoir
A capacitor bank that stores energy after rectification.
Standby
A switch state; it does not by itself prove the amplifier is de-energized.
Tank
The mechanical spring-reverb assembly.
Transformer
A magnetic device that transfers energy between windings.
Prototype
A test assembly used to check the design before release.
