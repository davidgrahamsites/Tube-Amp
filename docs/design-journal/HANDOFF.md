## CB-012 shunt-device charge response

CB-012 closes the proposed 3N50D2 shunt source-bias trial as inadequate. The IXYS DS100148D Fig. 13 typical curves show Coss about 2,350 pF at 0 V and 102 pF at 25 V, with Crss about 1,000 pF and 24 pF at those points. A square-root extrapolation, 102 × √26 ≈ 520 pF, is misleading: the capacitance is strongly voltage-dependent, and this shortcut does not represent charge through the transient. Claude independently checked the 6N50 PDF. The proposed source bias does not meet the protection requirement.

In the loaded quiet case, an opposed, shared-source 3N50 pair gives −0.928 dB with no bias. A −12 V negative-island source bias through 47 kΩ changes the result to −0.280 dB. This is an improvement in that operating point, not a transient-safety result. The independent nonlinear run applies curve-charge behavior at both gate/drain and source/drain, −12 V bias, unequal −2 V/−4.5 V thresholds, and twice the signal Miller term. ## CB-016 receiver-filter diode comparison

The first CB-016 screen uses 1.80 kΩ in series and 220 pF from the mixer to ground, with an 18.5 V drive and 40 ns ramp. The filter time constant is `τ = R × C = 1.80 kΩ × 220 pF = 396 ns`, nearly ten times the ramp. Its stored filter state therefore does not follow the drive edge immediately; removing a signal already playing when MUTE starts needs a separate low-impedance discharge path rather than relying on the resistor-capacitor branch alone.

Behavioral steering diodes bypass the resistor by polarity. With one diode per polarity, the quiet maximum under a −160 V step is 1.4069 V, while the maximum playing residual across two crest checks is 0.60844 V. Two diodes reduce the quiet maximum to 0.95472 V but raise the playing residual to 1.20726 V. Three reduce the quiet maximum to 0.70504 V while raising the playing residual to 1.84251 V. Thus each added diode moves the quiet and playing ceilings in opposite directions. Only the positive fault polarity was tested in playing cases; the parts are behavioral and no released diode is selected. This is a first screen, not a complete protection result. See the [CB-016 report](../../engineering/whole-amp/effects-loop/common-buffer/shunt-device.html#cb016) and [receiver-filter result](../../engineering/whole-amp/effects-loop/common-buffer/results/receiver-filter.json).

## CB-017 burst-memory mute-command comparison

The burst fixture applies a 135 V peak, 20 kHz source for four cycles, ends that source at zero, waits through a silence interval, then commands MUTE with a 40 ns smooth edge. This tests the charge that remains after a note ends. “Precommand mixer” is the mixer voltage immediately before the MUTE command. “Event peak” is the absolute mixer peak during the command response; it is not the residual after the event. “Post-100 ns” is the largest absolute mixer voltage from 100 ns after the command to the end of the run.

After 100 µs of silence, the static steering example at −9.75 V threshold and 1.1 charge scale has −48.083 V on the common source and −0.288 V at the mixer before MUTE. The absolute event peak is 4.014 V. A dynamic 12 V maximum reference gives −47.998 V common-source voltage and a 3.937 V absolute event peak in the corresponding run. These pulse heights must not be described as post-event residuals.

With a 1 MΩ return and dynamic reference, the 1 ms silence case has +0.134 V common-source voltage before MUTE and a 1.423 V absolute event peak. At 10 ms silence, the absolute event peak remains 1.384 V, above the 0.70 V screen. The bleed restores the source voltage. The remaining mute pulse still exceeds the quiet limit. ## CB-019 bridge-reservoir and charger-loss comparison

CB-019 separates loss of the isolated charger from a direct short across the local bias. One remaining Panasonic EEUFR1A332 is modeled at its 2,640 µF minimum. With constant 165 mA for 3.01 ms, `ΔV = I × t / C = 0.165 A × 0.00301 s / 0.002640 F ≈ 0.188 V`. Bulk capacitance provides hold-up after charger loss; it does not clamp the bridge’s instantaneous voltage during the fast MUTE edge.

With eight 1 µF effective ceramic paths, one minimum bulk capacitor, and 0.25 nH common inductance, the 2.0 V initial-state run samples all four sign/crest combinations at 1 ns. The bridge reaches at least 1.674 V; bulk finishes at or above 1.785 V, and the largest mixer playing residual is 0.680 V. Starting at 1.8 V, the same setup reaches 1.465 V at the bridge. Across all reserve configurations, 29 of 36 runs completed. One of the four 0.25 ns refinements completed at the 2.0 V starting charge.

A dead short directly across local bias is a separate CB-018 failure; stored charge cannot fix that fault. In the 2.0 V recheck, 14 loaded tones have worst THD of 0.565% (rounded upward), and nine quiet cases reach at most 0.645 V. Keep the 1 V playing-residual and 0.70 V quiet-peak criteria distinct.
