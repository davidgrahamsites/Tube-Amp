Absolute output protection authority

November 26, 2026 · HD-008 · Forced-on DC screen

Question and scope

HD-007 screened two series pass devices against one device short. HD-008 asks whether a control path can force the output below 300 V and whether a detector has a usable trip window.

The 192 DC cases use three actuator modes, two input voltages, two regulator profiles, warm or unpowered-tube load, two MOSFET cutoff sensitivities per device, and two source-resistor values. The actuator is forced on. There is no automatic detector, LED supply, delay, transient protection, thermal proof, or physical test in these cases.

The upper-reference shunt does not control the two worst sampled cases. Direct sinks have more authority in this model. These are forced-on equilibria. They do not prove an automatic trigger, response time, or peak-voltage limit.

The MOSFET VTO of −5 V is an allocated cutoff sensitivity. It is not a guaranteed hot device limit. The gate clamps and pass devices remain generic models.

Detector window has no overlap

The maximum normal output calculated from the unchanged HD-006 programming profile is 292.156 V. The proposed ceiling is 300 V. A TL431BI reference has a 2.483–2.507 V initial range at 25 °C, at 10 mA cathode current, plus up to 34 mV full-temperature deviation in the cited specification. The screen uses those endpoints before adding divider, bias, or transient errors.

\[r_{min}=\frac{2.483-0.034}{2.495}=0.981563,\qquad r_{max}=\frac{2.507+0.034}{2.495}=1.018437\]
\[V_{trip,min}=\frac{292.156}{r_{min}}=297.644\;\mathrm{V},\qquad V_{trip,max}=\frac{300}{r_{max}}=294.569\;\mathrm{V}\]

A nominal threshold must be above 297.644 V to avoid a trip at the screened normal maximum. It must be below 294.569 V to trip by the 300 V ceiling. The requirements conflict:

\[297.644\;\mathrm{V}>294.569\;\mathrm{V}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{no feasible nominal trip point}\]

This is a necessary-window calculation only. It has no allowance for divider error, detector bias, noise, hysteresis, startup, or overshoot. The detector as bounded here cannot meet both requirements.

Decision and next comparison

No detector or actuator is adopted. The direct-sink cases show a control-authority boundary, not a protection circuit. The next comparison is an exact detector tolerance stack against a lower regulator setpoint. Do not adopt the detector until the normal and trip windows overlap with margin.

Verify the detector supply during startup, faults, and input collapse. Then check exact clamp and pass-device behavior, hot MOSFET limits, transient overshoot, reverse paths, isolation, thermal design, socket/carrier, and populated fit. No automatic protection or hardware qualification is established.

Vocabulary

Actuator
The control element that changes the regulator reference or pass-device state.
Forced-on case
A model run that commands the actuator on without modeling the detector that would trigger it.
Upper-reference shunt
A 100 Ω path across each upper reference-divider resistor; the reference still controls the gate above the output.
Direct sink
A modeled current sink connected directly to one or both regulator reference nodes.
Cutoff sensitivity
A selected transistor threshold value used to explore model response. It is not a guaranteed device limit.
Detector window
The range of nominal trip values that must satisfy normal-operation and maximum-voltage requirements.
Full-temperature deviation
The specified change in reference voltage across the stated temperature range.
Hysteresis
A difference between the voltage that turns protection on and the voltage that lets it turn off.
Transient overshoot
A temporary voltage peak above the steady-state value during a change or fault.
