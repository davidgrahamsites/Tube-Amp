Harness header datum and mated envelope

November 26, 2026 · HI-004 · Nominal geometry checked; assembled fit remains open

Question and source review

HI-004 resolves the board-edge datum for the 4-circuit header and separates header clearance from the plugged-in harness envelope. It uses Molex drawing SD-43045-001 for header 430450402, whose geometry is shared with 430450400, and housing 430250400.

The downloaded header drawing is Rev G from LCSC. Its page 1 whole view and enlarged location detail were reviewed; page 2 duplicates page 1. The current manufacturer drawing is Rev H1. Only its text was checked. Common nominal dimensions and the edge note agree, but a full visual revision comparison remains open. The housing drawing is Rev A. Its whole sheet, housing-size table, and right-angle mated view were visually reviewed. The manufacturer's text shows the same revision.

Edge datum and placement limit

The 10.16 mm maximum is measured along the insertion axis from the mating-side PCB edge to the locator-peg centre. It is not measured from the header face or signal-pin row. In the installed footprint coordinates, the peg centre is at \(y=-4.32\;\mathrm{mm}\). Therefore the mating-side edge must satisfy the relation below. This is a necessary placement limit only. The board edge must not cut through the locator peg.

\[y_{edge}\ge y_{peg}-10.16\;\mathrm{mm}=-4.32\;\mathrm{mm}-10.16\;\mathrm{mm}=-14.48\;\mathrm{mm}\]

Six library geometry comparisons and four edge-control checks pass. The checks compare the peg, front, rear and stepped width with the library, compare the edge limit with the contract, and identify the mate extension. They do not check a mating connector on any board. The native board hashes are preserved; zero actual placements were checked.

Why the header footprint is not enough

The header front face is at \(y=-8.92\;\mathrm{mm}\); its rear body is at \(y=0.99\;\mathrm{mm}\). The mated wire-exit face is at \(y=-16.57\;\mathrm{mm}\). It extends 7.65 mm beyond the header front face. At the limiting edge position, that nominal exit face lies 2.09 mm past the board edge. This is a rigid-body comparison, not a clearance approval.

\[L_{extension}=|-16.57-(-8.92)|=7.65\;\mathrm{mm}\]
\[d_{past-edge}=|-16.57-(-14.48)|=2.09\;\mathrm{mm}\]

The family right-angle mated drawing gives 17.56 mm axial length and 10.81 mm height. The axial length runs from the wire-exit face to the header rear body. The drawing also calls out 11.0 mm latching height. The values do not include wire bend radius, withdrawal stroke, or finger access. No top pull tab is available.

A matching footprint or courtyard checks only the header body and its board interface. It does not reserve space for the housing, latch, harness bend, unplugging motion, fingers, or nearby chassis parts. The current nominal drawing comparison cannot establish service access or assembled fit.

Status and required checks

No board placement or native change was made. Full tolerance-envelope qualification, a visual Rev H1 comparison, mating-face harness numbering, wire bend radius, withdrawal stroke, finger access, exact revision confirmation, and populated-assembly fit remain open. Header pin maximum section, finished-hole capability, and electrical insulation remain separate checks.

Before fit approval, confirm exact part revisions and cavity markings. Mate an insulated sample harness in the populated assembly. Measure latch access, wire bend, withdrawal, and nearby clearances. Record the measurement method and actual part markings. The 10.16 mm edge limit alone is not fit approval.

Vocabulary

Locator peg
A molded post that locates the header housing on the board. Keep it intact.
Mating-side edge
The board edge nearest the connector insertion direction.
Mated envelope
The combined nominal dimensions of the header and plugged-in housing.
Wire-exit face
The face where the wires leave the right-angle housing.
Courtyard
A PCB layout outline used to reserve nominal component space. It does not include service access unless separately defined.
Withdrawal stroke
The free travel needed to unplug the housing from the header.
Finished-hole capability
The achievable diameter and tolerance of a plated PCB hole after manufacture.
Native board
The project's editable PCB design, distinct from a drawing or simulation.
