Composite recovery stage feasibility

November 26–27, 2026 · CB-002–004 and FB-176/178 · Model and partial measurement evidence only; no device or circuit adopted

Question and test limits

Can a low-voltage error amplifier control a discrete high-voltage gain stage and follower for the recovery output? The tests check feedback compensation, transistor variation, output current, load capacitance, distortion and estimated heat. It does not alter the active circuit or select parts.

All 18 test cases completed. Seven included tests over time. The saved circuit files did not change. A smaller time step gave matching results in the repeated case. The independent review passes 75 checks and rejects three deliberately corrupted records. These checks do not approve the hardware.

Compensation choices

The 4.7 pF nominal compensation case has 58.93° phase margin. It meets the 45° screen in that model. With no compensation, margin falls to −13.15°; reject that case. A 47 pF value is too slow: gain at 20 kHz falls 2.605 dB, outside the 0.5 dB screen.

A 10 pF candidate gives 102.42° nominal margin. A strong generic-MOS sensitivity case gives 46.20°. In the original CB-002 run, three cases did not reach a valid steady-state result: low threshold, a repeated seeded low-threshold case, and the weak 10 pF case. The external starting guesses did not fix those results. The CB-003 section below records the later repair. The nominal 10 pF result alone does not approve the design.

The tests use a simple MOSFET model written for this study. Its current follows a square-law equation. The IDSS and threshold values are chosen test values. It omits temperature effects, breakdown, the body diode, changes in capacitance with voltage and safe operating area. It also does not show how production variations occur together. The archived TI OPAx134 macro-model is typical behavior, not guaranteed limits.

Replace the ideal sink

The baseline uses an ideal 2 mA sink. Two follow-up cases replace it with a second OPAx134 model, a third generic MOSFET, a 499 Ω sense resistor, and an ideal 1 V reference. This checks the servo idea only. The amplifier, MOSFET, reference, protection, and startup are not selected or qualified.

Capacitance and current limit

For a sinusoidal load, the capacitive current is \(I_C=2\pi fCV_{pk}\). At 20 kHz, the screen gives a necessary load-capacitance bound of \(115.545891\;\mathrm{pF}\) for the 2 mA sink. Under the same test convention, 100 pF requires 1.7423066 mA; 500 pF requires 8.4915883 mA.

The 500 pF run uses the ideal sink. It drives the generic MOS gate-source voltage to 286.16 V, far outside the 20 V model screen. The solver also reports 33.447 W peak follower dissipation. That value is outside the model domain. It is not a physical power prediction.

Thermal estimate

The 20 kHz servo case reports 0.40703 W average in the follower. Using the assumed 70 °C ambient and typical 132 °C/W junction-to-air resistance:

\[T_J\approx T_A+\theta_{JA}P=70+132(0.40703)=123.73\;^\circ\mathrm C\]

This is about 1.27 °C below the assumed 125 °C design ceiling. Typical thermal resistance varies with board and mounting conditions. This small modeled margin is not thermal approval.

Decision and next checks

Keep the composite stage as a feasibility candidate only. Do not add it to native CAD or the build. Next, choose supported amplifier, MOSFET, sink, reference, and protection parts. Check valid startup and operating points across temperature and device limits. Re-run the complete current and thermal stage with the real source impedance, reference, feedback network, cable, and integrated loop load. Check gate protection, output faults, and safe operating area. Test the low-voltage controls separately before powered high-voltage tests.

CB-003 operating-point follow-up

Three CB-002 operating-point runs were invalid with the original solver setup: low-threshold closed loop, seeded low-threshold closed loop, and weak 10 pF return ratio. CB-003 tested five selected cases twice: once with the original setup and once with internal-node guesses. Each case used a closed-loop and return-ratio run, for 20 runs total. The original failures remain recorded.

The solver used 118 internal op-amp node voltages from the valid nominal result as starting guesses. Ngspice releases these .nodeset guesses while solving. They steer the first numerical search; they do not force the final voltages. No vendor model was edited. No tolerance was relaxed. No UIC startup mode or hardware change was used.

All ten guided fixtures found valid operating points. Each paired voltage result agrees within 1 mV. The largest current-balance error among accepted results is \(2.83157\;\mathrm{nA}\). The independent audit passes 69 checks and rejects three deliberately corrupted records. The starting guesses affected the earlier numerical result. These tests do not prove startup or the behavior of real parts.

CB-004 output current and heat screen

This calculation asks how much output current and shared heat sinking a two-device class-A output pair may need. The 135 V peak at 20 kHz is an overload stress case. It is not the normal 25 W clean-output requirement. The 100 pF, 250 pF, and 500 pF values are load scenarios. They are not measured wiring capacitance.

For a resistive load in parallel with capacitance, calculate peak load current from the resistor and capacitor currents, which differ in phase by 90 degrees:

\[I_{load,pk}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{V_{pk}}{R_L}\right)^2+\left(2\pi f C_L V_{pk}\right)^2}\]

Use \(V_{pk}=135\;\mathrm V\), \(R_L=340\;\mathrm{k\Omega}\), and \(f=20\;\mathrm{kHz}\). The resistor current is \(135/340{,}000=0.397059\;\mathrm{mA}\). The calculated total peaks are:

Load capacitancePeak load current2 mA bias minus peak
100 pF1.742307 mA+0.257693 mA
250 pF4.259696 mA−2.259696 mA
500 pF8.491588 mA−6.491588 mA

This is a load-only current floor. It excludes feedback current, gate charging, bias variation, protection, and faults. The 2 mA bias clears the 100 pF scenario only. It does not clear 250 pF or 500 pF. At 500 pF, a 13 mA trial bias leaves \(13-8.491588=4.508412\;\mathrm{mA}\) above this load-current floor. This is not a chosen bias or a complete current rating.

At the stated 200 V output bias and 400 V supply, the 13 mA no-signal standing losses are:

\[P_{follower}=(400-200)(0.013)=2.6\;\mathrm W,\quad P_{sink}=200(0.013)=2.6\;\mathrm W,\quad P_{pair}=5.2\;\mathrm W\]

The model allocates a shared sink with 70 °C ambient, a 125 °C junction design target, and an assumed 2 °C/W insulating interface. For each device, calculate local case-to-junction rise from its 2.6 W standing loss, then allocate the remaining rise across the shared sink:

\[\theta_{SA,max}=\frac{T_{J,target}-T_A-P_{device}(\theta_{JC}+\theta_{interface})}{P_{pair}}\]

For the IRF820 example, the local rise is \(2.6(2.5+2)=11.7\;^\circ\mathrm C\); the remaining sink budget is \((125-70-11.7)/5.2=8.3269\;^\circ\mathrm C/\mathrm W\). For the IXTP01N100D, it is \((125-70-2.6(5+2))/5.2=7.0769\;^\circ\mathrm C/\mathrm W\). The insulating interface value is assumed. These ceilings reach the junction target exactly. Select a lower sink resistance after mounting tolerances, enclosure airflow and other shared heat are known. Neither result selects a device or heat sink.

The calculation receipt passes 44 checks. These examples do not qualify safe operating area, DC or hot linear operation, exact sourcing, tab insulation, mounting fit, bias tolerance, or shared heat from nearby parts. No device is selected. Native CAD is unchanged.

FB-176/178 drill measurements

FB-176 reported 21 cm from the drill-bit tip to the tool back and 28 mm from the bit to the chuck. The first value converts to \(21\;\mathrm{cm}\times10=210\;\mathrm{mm}\). FB-176 also reported a 39 mm widest chuck. FB-178 clarified that the widest chuck diameter is 46.5 mm and the motor housing is 51 mm wide. This resolves the earlier chuck conflict; the 39 mm report remains part of the measurement history.

With the proposed 2 mm clearance allowance on each side, the chuck cross-section is \(46.5+2(2)=50.5\;\mathrm{mm}\). The corresponding axis-to-obstruction distance is \(46.5/2+2=25.25\;\mathrm{mm}\), only where the obstruction overlaps the chuck axially. For the motor housing, the same allowance gives \(51+2(2)=55\;\mathrm{mm}\) across its width. Do not treat the housing as a circular envelope centered on the drill axis.

The exact tool model, measurement setup, and uncertainty are not verified. Housing height and axis offset, chuck axial length, and fastener positions remain unknown. Full approach, withdrawal, stabilization, and screw access remain unverified. These dimensions do not establish swept clearance or a pass.

Vocabulary

Compensation
Parts used to control feedback response and reduce the risk of oscillation.
IDSS
Drain current at zero gate-to-source voltage under stated test conditions.
Threshold voltage
A model or data-sheet value used to describe when transistor conduction changes.
Square-law model
A simplified transistor model in which current depends on the square of a voltage difference.
Current-balance error
The difference between currents entering and leaving a connection point.
Safe operating area
Allowed combinations of voltage, current, temperature and time for a device.
AC
Alternating current; a small-signal frequency test in this record.
Compensation
A capacitor or network used to control feedback-loop response.
DC operating point
The steady voltage and current values before a signal is applied.
Generic MOSFET model
An authored transistor approximation. It is not a manufacturer model or selected device.
Ideal sink
A simulated current draw that holds its value without real-device limits.
Phase margin
A feedback-stability measure at the frequency where loop gain reaches unity.
Servo sink
A feedback circuit that adjusts transistor current using a sensed voltage.
THD
Total harmonic distortion; harmonic signal content relative to the fundamental.
Transient run
A time-domain simulation of changing voltages and currents.
Valid model domain
The voltage and current range where the model represents supported behavior.
Internal node guess
A starting estimate for a simulator's numerical solution. It does not set or force the final voltage.
.nodeset
An ngspice command that gives initial voltage guesses while allowing the solver to find its own final operating point.
Load-only current floor
Minimum peak output current calculated for the stated load alone. It excludes feedback, gate charging, tolerances, and faults.
Standing loss
Power dissipated at the stated DC bias before the output signal is applied.
Sink-to-air thermal resistance
Temperature rise of the shared heat sink divided by its total heat flow to air, in °C/W.
Motor-body offset
Distance between the drill axis and the outer motor housing.
