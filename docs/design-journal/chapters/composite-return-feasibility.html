Composite recovery stage feasibility

November 26–28, 2026 · CB-002–007 and FB-176/178 · Model and partial measurement evidence only; no device or circuit adopted

Question and test limits

Can a low-voltage error amplifier control a discrete high-voltage gain stage and follower for the recovery output? The tests check feedback compensation, transistor variation, output current, load capacitance, distortion and estimated heat. It does not alter the active circuit or select parts.

All 18 test cases completed. Seven included tests over time. The saved circuit files did not change. A smaller time step gave matching results in the repeated case. The independent review passes 75 checks and rejects three deliberately corrupted records. These checks do not approve the hardware.

Compensation choices

The 4.7 pF nominal compensation case has 58.93° phase margin. It meets the 45° screen in that model. With no compensation, margin falls to −13.15°; reject that case. A 47 pF value is too slow: gain at 20 kHz falls 2.605 dB, outside the 0.5 dB screen.

A 10 pF candidate gives 102.42° nominal margin. A strong generic-MOS sensitivity case gives 46.20°. In the original CB-002 run, three cases did not reach a valid steady-state result: low threshold, a repeated seeded low-threshold case, and the weak 10 pF case. The external starting guesses did not fix those results. The CB-003 section below records the later repair. The nominal 10 pF result alone does not approve the design.

The tests use a simple MOSFET model written for this study. Its current follows a square-law equation. The IDSS and threshold values are chosen test values. It omits temperature effects, breakdown, the body diode, changes in capacitance with voltage and safe operating area. It also does not show how production variations occur together. The archived TI OPAx134 macro-model is typical behavior, not guaranteed limits.

Replace the ideal sink

The baseline uses an ideal 2 mA sink. Two follow-up cases replace it with a second OPAx134 model, a third generic MOSFET, a 499 Ω sense resistor, and an ideal 1 V reference. This checks the servo idea only. The amplifier, MOSFET, reference, protection, and startup are not selected or qualified.

Capacitance and current limit

For a sinusoidal load, the capacitive current is \(I_C=2\pi fCV_{pk}\). At 20 kHz, the screen gives a necessary load-capacitance bound of \(115.545891\;\mathrm{pF}\) for the 2 mA sink. Under the same test convention, 100 pF requires 1.7423066 mA; 500 pF requires 8.4915883 mA.

The 500 pF run uses the ideal sink. It drives the generic MOS gate-source voltage to 286.16 V, far outside the 20 V model screen. The solver also reports 33.447 W peak follower dissipation. That value is outside the model domain. It is not a physical power prediction.

Thermal estimate

The 20 kHz servo case reports 0.40703 W average in the follower. Using the assumed 70 °C ambient and typical 132 °C/W junction-to-air resistance:

\[T_J\approx T_A+\theta_{JA}P=70+132(0.40703)=123.73\;^\circ\mathrm C\]

This is about 1.27 °C below the assumed 125 °C design ceiling. Typical thermal resistance varies with board and mounting conditions. This small modeled margin is not thermal approval.

Decision and next checks

Keep the composite stage as a feasibility candidate only. Do not add it to native CAD or the build. Next, choose supported amplifier, MOSFET, sink, reference, and protection parts. Check valid startup and operating points across temperature and device limits. Re-run the complete current and thermal stage with the real source impedance, reference, feedback network, cable, and integrated loop load. Check gate protection, output faults, and safe operating area. Test the low-voltage controls separately before powered high-voltage tests.

The original failures remain recorded.

The solver used 118 internal op-amp node voltages from the valid nominal result as starting guesses. Ngspice releases these .nodeset guesses while solving. They steer the first numerical search; they do not force the final voltages. No vendor model was edited. No tolerance was relaxed. No UIC startup mode or hardware change was used.

The independent audit passes 69 checks and rejects three deliberately corrupted records. The starting guesses affected the earlier numerical result. These tests do not prove startup or the behavior of real parts.

CB-004 output current and heat screen

This calculation asks how much output current and shared heat sinking a two-device class-A output pair may need. The 135 V peak at 20 kHz is an overload stress case. It is not the normal 25 W clean-output requirement. The 100 pF, 250 pF, and 500 pF values are load scenarios. They are not measured wiring capacitance.

For a resistive load in parallel with capacitance, calculate peak load current from the resistor and capacitor currents, which differ in phase by 90 degrees:

\[I_{load,pk}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{V_{pk}}{R_L}\right)^2+\left(2\pi f C_L V_{pk}\right)^2}\]

Use \(V_{pk}=135\;\mathrm V\), \(R_L=340\;\mathrm{k\Omega}\), and \(f=20\;\mathrm{kHz}\). The resistor current is \(135/340{,}000=0.397059\;\mathrm{mA}\). The calculated total peaks are:

Load capacitancePeak load current2 mA bias minus peak
100 pF1.742307 mA+0.257693 mA
250 pF4.259696 mA−2.259696 mA
500 pF8.491588 mA−6.491588 mA

This is a load-only current floor. It excludes feedback current, gate charging, bias variation, protection, and faults. The 2 mA bias clears the 100 pF scenario only. It does not clear 250 pF or 500 pF. At 500 pF, a 13 mA trial bias leaves \(13-8.491588=4.508412\;\mathrm{mA}\) above this load-current floor. This is not a chosen bias or a complete current rating.

At the stated 200 V output bias and 400 V supply, the 13 mA no-signal standing losses are:

\[P_{follower}=(400-200)(0.013)=2.6\;\mathrm W,\quad P_{sink}=200(0.013)=2.6\;\mathrm W,\quad P_{pair}=5.2\;\mathrm W\]

The model allocates a shared sink with 70 °C ambient, a 125 °C junction design target, and an assumed 2 °C/W insulating interface. For each device, calculate local case-to-junction rise from its 2.6 W standing loss, then allocate the remaining rise across the shared sink:

\[\theta_{SA,max}=\frac{T_{J,target}-T_A-P_{device}(\theta_{JC}+\theta_{interface})}{P_{pair}}\]
Illustrative leadMaximum \(\theta_{JC}\)Calculated common sink-to-air ceiling
IRF820PbF2.5 °C/W8.3269 °C/W
IXTP01N100D5 °C/W7.0769 °C/W

For the IRF820 example, the local rise is \(2.6(2.5+2)=11.7\;^\circ\mathrm C\); the remaining sink budget is \((125-70-11.7)/5.2=8.3269\;^\circ\mathrm C/\mathrm W\). For the IXTP01N100D, it is \((125-70-2.6(5+2))/5.2=7.0769\;^\circ\mathrm C/\mathrm W\). The insulating interface value is assumed. These ceilings reach the junction target exactly. Select a lower sink resistance after mounting tolerances, enclosure airflow and other shared heat are known. Neither result selects a device or heat sink.

The calculation receipt passes 44 checks. These examples do not qualify safe operating area, DC or hot linear operation, exact sourcing, tab insulation, mounting fit, bias tolerance, or shared heat from nearby parts. No device is selected. Native CAD is unchanged.

CB-005 integrated load and transient check

CB-005 connected the composite-return model to the integrated effects-loop load. This checks whether the earlier standalone output-load assumptions describe the modeled port. The results remain simulation evidence. The model omits the active wet signal, clamps, and physical wiring.

All 36 port-probe configurations returned the same admittance at 20 kHz: \(Y=G+jB=(2.567509+j1.636218)\;\mathrm{\mu S}\). The equivalent impedance is \(Z=276990-j176520\;\Omega\). At 1 kHz, the equivalent impedance is \(483116-j22410\;\Omega\).

For added external capacitance \(C\), frequency \(f\), and peak output voltage \(V_{pk}\), calculate external current from the magnitude of the total admittance:

\[I_{ext,pk}=V_{pk}\left|Y+j2\pi fC\right|=V_{pk}\sqrt{G^2+(B+2\pi fC)^2}\]

Use \(V_{pk}=135\;\mathrm V\), \(f=20\;\mathrm{kHz}\), \(G=2.567509\;\mathrm{\mu S}\), and \(B=1.636218\;\mathrm{\mu S}\). The calculated external-only peak currents are:

Added capacitanceExternal peak current
0 pF0.411015 mA
100 pF1.948428 mA
500 pF8.710089 mA

For an ideal 2 mA external-only limit, solve \(C=(\sqrt{(I_{lim}/V_{pk})^2-G^2}-B)/(2\pi f)\). Substituting \(I_{lim}=2\;\mathrm{mA}\) gives \(C=103.088\;\mathrm{pF}\). This crossing allocates no current to internal feedback or other paths and includes no margin. It is not a harness-capacitance specification or a device-current qualification.

At 1 kHz, the 4.27 V target produced 4.2695, 4.2691, and 4.2674 V across 0, 100, and 500 pF, with 0.0020–0.0022% THD. The 135 V runs are overload stresses and exceed the nominal spring-send input limit; they produced 134.985, 134.972, and 134.917 V. At the 20 kHz, 135 V overload stress, completed 0 and 100 pF cases produced 131.334 V at 1.1417% THD and 131.382 V at 1.1595% THD. Both exceed the 1% screen. The 500 pF run stopped at 36.22 µs of the requested 3 ms after an ngspice timestep abort; no valid transient peak or THD is available for that case. A separate 30 ms downstream forced-port control at 135 V peak and 20 kHz measured −8.590/+8.494 mA, with branch-current sum error within 5 µA; it establishes external current demand for the prescribed waveform, not recovery performance.

CB-005 resolves the nominal integrated load for this model and adds an external-current screen. It does not close continuous hot-device capability, safe operating area, startup, gate protection, fault current, internal feedback current, or thermal performance. No part or circuit is adopted. Continue with supported device and protection choices, then rerun the complete integrated current and thermal cases before low-voltage fixture testing.

CB-006 single-rail Apex return trials

CB-006 tests an Apex PA340CC with an OPA134-family low-voltage stage on the CB-005 downstream model. The initial trial assumes one 310 V rail, 3.3 kΩ output isolation, low-voltage gain 4.16, high-voltage AC gain 25, and a 1.37 MΩ R601 spring-send trial value. It is a model experiment, not an adopted stage.

At the CB-005 500 pF, 20 kHz, 135 V peak overload target, the initial trial delivered 134.701 V peak at 0.2915% THD and 9.2095 V peak at SEND_IN. A finer, longer simulation delivered 134.804 V peak at 0.2592% THD. At the 4.27 V, 1 kHz target, it delivered 4.3771 V peak at 0.0084% THD. The latter is a spring-send recalibration signal; R601 remains a trial value. At equal recovery output, the R601/R602 trial changes spring excitation by \(11/14.7=0.748\), or \(20\log_{10}(11/14.7)=-2.52\;\mathrm{dB}\). Recalibrate the complete spring circuit. These are model outputs using an assumed supply and candidate amplifier models.

The PA341DW alternative costs more in the compared parts. Its pin 7 provides output current limiting. The PA340CC is a seven-lead DDPAK without output current limiting; its tab is connected to −Vs. The package and protection differences affect the carrier, insulation and heat path. The TI OPA134PA Rev B pin table marks pins 1 and 5 NC and pin 8 floating; use that revision when wiring a socket (TI OPA134 data sheet). Neither part is selected.

The guarded-input revision raises the OPA134-family gain to 5.02 and adds two 24 kΩ series resistors in the normal divider and fault-injection paths. It delivers 9.27 V peak at SEND_IN against the 10 V design limit; this does not close spring-drive qualification. With the same 310 V, 3.3 kΩ isolation, 500 pF, 20 kHz, 135 V target and 1.37 MΩ R601 trial, it produced 135.536 V peak at 0.2922% THD; the finer run produced 135.640 V at 0.2599%. The 4.27 V, 1 kHz case produced 4.405 V peak. A soft 279 V source model fell to 276.009 V and produced 1.3669% THD, above the 1% screen. In the 320 V, 85 °C short case, model chip loss reached 2.044 W and the isolation resistor reached 2.989 W. These are simulated cases, not fault or thermal qualification.

For a sine of frequency \(f\) and peak voltage \(V_{pk}\), the minimum required slew rate is:

\[SR=2\pi fV_{pk}=2\pi(20{,}000\;\mathrm{Hz})(135\;\mathrm V)=16.965\;\mathrm{V/\mu s}\]

The initial hot model case reports 1.2538 W chip dissipation at a 320 V rail and 85 °C. With 70 °C ambient and an 85 °C maximum case temperature, the case-to-air allowance is:

\[\theta_{CA,max}=\frac{T_{case,max}-T_A}{P}=\frac{85-70}{1.2538}=11.96\;\mathrm{K/W}\]

This is the initial nominal model-based thermal ceiling. The guarded follow-up adds a conservative \(P_R=320\;\mathrm V\times2.5\;\mathrm{mA}=0.800\;\mathrm W\) reserve to the modeled loss. The source report deliberately double-counts some idle loss. The budgets are \(15/(1.2554+0.800)=7.30\;\mathrm{K/W}\) in overload and \(15/(2.044+0.800)=5.27\;\mathrm{K/W}\) for the port short. Neither budget establishes a selected carrier, socket, interface or measured heat path. Raw rail loss, startup and mute behavior, actual carrier/socket/insulation/heat, and spring-send calibration remain open. The fault-budget calculation gives 0.565 mA from the intact divider and 6.667 mA for a divider rail-short. The two 24 kΩ resistors bound these modeled input-injection cases; they do not qualify the output fault path or the amplifier. A PA340 pass-device short to the 440 V raw rail would exceed its absolute maximum by 90 V; an independent rail disconnect remains unresolved.

CB-007 supply cutoff, startup and mute sequence

CB-007 couples an analog supply regulator and overvoltage cutoff to the CB-006 PA340 return model. The supply/audio, loop, mute and fault receipts are simulations. No hardware or placement is approved. See the readable calculation entry and the engineering report.

The comparator trip-corner calculation spans 325.683–338.028 V. The prescribed-delay RC calculation gives 344.515 V with one 17.6 µF reservoir and 2 ms delay, and 366.589 V at 10 ms. The updated LM2903BIDR detector/actuator model gives 343.533 V at its slowest threshold with 1.9 ms actuation and 15.8 µs comparator delay; extending actuation to 10 ms gives 365.255 V. Both-reservoir loss with only 0.1 µF bypass or a welded cutoff approaches 440 V. The simulation is not a timing measurement.

The regulator feedback change replaces the 10 nF path with 100 nF and 100 kΩ in series. Sampled loop margins rise from 10.308–20.459° to 73.769–83.671°. Nominal, low-line and high-hot audio cases hold near 309.5 V. The 290 V brownout case falls to 272.420–272.488 V and gives 1.611% THD, above the 1% screen. The 440 V high-hot case with precharge resistance in circuit gives 134.848 V peak at 0.240% THD. Reject the unguided bypass-weld transition waveform: its solver starts at 376.332 V and samples 374.652–374.789 V.

The cold-start transient cases abort at time zero in the OPA134 model. Powered-start cases initialized at 100 V estimate mute release at 1.985 s and 1.778 s; a gain-capacitor-short case stays muted through 5 s. A coarse 3 ms relay-opening race gives a 154.798 V mixer peak for simultaneous onset or a welded contact. In the refined optical-switch model, a 5 ms warning lead and 4.7 Ω receiver shunt reduce the peak to 0.197 V at 30 pF or 0.912 V at 150 pF. A stuck series switch gives 2.564 V, and no warning lead gives 154.226 V. Optical play-drive cases give 134.031 V peak/0.239% THD at 30 pF per shunt and 129.526 V/0.210% at 150 pF. These are model comparisons, not a selected mute implementation.

All four zero-state startup fixtures fail at time zero; the powered-start fixtures do not establish startup from zero. The revised bypass closes below 140 V differential and opens above 160 V. Its modeled closure times are 1.144 s at 340 V raw, 1.473 s at 390 V, and 1.825 s at 440 V. At 400 V raw, a 1.4 s sustained load with bypass open bottoms at 283.50 V; bypass closed remains above 309.6 V. A pass short before ARM reaches 340.238 V with 80.972 mA worst modeled feed for 52.8 µF total capacitance. The 12 V coil option puts all 72 modeled auxiliary cases above the provisional 0.25 A half-winding screen (maximum 0.4566 A). A three-switch optical option still places 48/72 cases above it (maximum 0.3825 A; 56 cases flagged). The screen is not a transformer rating. Seven state-logic injections pass their modeled sequence checks only.

The revised thermal calculation assumes 60 °C effective external-wall air and 5 K/W wall-to-case resistance. With the 0.800 W reserve, full-sine loss gives \(60+(1.2554+0.800)5=70.28\;^\circ\mathrm C\); port-short loss gives \(60+(2.044+0.800)5=74.22\;^\circ\mathrm C\). Both are below the nominal 79 °C trip (±3 °C). The prior 70 °C case baseline is rejected because it predicted 80.28 °C full-sine temperature above nominal trip. A replacement 60 °C transient with degraded fins and joint peaks at 82.15 °C (attached sensor, 1 s lag) or 83.17 °C (8 s lag); a detached sink-reading sensor fails to trip within 300 s while the case reaches 91.08 °C. These are model results. The revised wall temperature and path remain assumptions. No placement or hardware is approved.

Readable equations and assumptions are in the CB-007 calculation entry. Nine result receipts, including detector/actuator cases, are hash-listed by the audit receipt.

CB-008 diode detector and sudden-loss guard

CB-008 adds a modeled fast detector and switched receiver shunt to the CB-007 optical-mute path. These are circuit simulations and bounded envelope checks, not a selected or tested protection assembly. See the CB-008 engineering report.

The divider/detector sweep now has 29 configurations, including five slow-ramp pin-only cases, quiet operation, both fault polarities and missed-detection cases. With 7 pF detector-input parasitic, fast-fault detector excursions are about 2.45–2.56 V. The receiver-switch model allocates 0.40 µs after first threshold crossing; representative fast cases close by 0.421 µs total and produce about 0.894–0.902 V at the modeled mixer. This post-crossing interval is an assumed detector/latch/gate allocation, not measured hardware latency. A valid 20 kHz sine does not trip the edge detector; a slow output upset with healthy rails can escape.

The 28-case sudden-fault network uses an assumed 10 Ω receiver shunt, 500 pF harness, 0.35 pF feedthrough and modeled delay line. In step-only (“silent”) cases, the nominal 0.5 µs trigger gives 0.500 V peak. A 25 µs missed-detector delay reaches 177.613 V before switching; the post-switch peak is 0.402 V. Playing cases add a 160 V fault step to a 135 V peak, 20 kHz sine. Across both fault polarities and four sine phases, pre-switch peaks span 83.193–114.996 V; post-switch peaks span 0.522–0.896 V. The unbounded resistive shunt model demands up to 10.92 A during discharge. A separate 3 A-limited model still settles below 0.90 V, but peak device current, transient safe operating area and audible interruption remain unverified.

The 60 ms handover model gives 0.913 V peak and 0.028 V at 3 ms after a ±160 V step with up to 150 pF across the optical contacts. A welded series contact gives 0.288 V at 3 ms. An open fast shunt lets 172.737 V through at onset, while the healthy opening optical contact sees 346.727 V. The prior CB-007 154.798 V coarse relay-race result and 154.226 V no-warning optical result remain outcomes from different mute-network models, not direct before/after comparators.

The 15-row full audio model is within the ±0.50 dB gain screen at the representative 390 V raw case (140.730 V peak, +0.361 dB, 0.329% THD). With the 39.7 kΩ feedback arm untrimmed, the high-temperature, −10% inductance corner with a 500 pF harness, 600 pF shunt and 150 pF open-optical capacitance gives −0.572 dB and fails the lower gain limit; a 1 nF shunt sensitivity gives −0.893 dB. A per-carrier trim to 40.6 kΩ brings that −10% corner to −0.417 dB; a separate +10% inductance trim to 38.3 kΩ gives +0.300 dB. With 14 mA sustained load and a 340 V raw trough, the extended regulator settles at 297.94–298.03 V; at 390 V raw the same load remains above 309.69 V. The 16.5 mA low-line stress falls to about 255.30 V and calls for low-rail mute. The hot PA port-short model dissipates 2.161 W; adding the retained 0.800 W reserve gives \(2.161+0.800=2.961\;\mathrm W\). Under the report's assumed 60 °C air and 4+1 K/W path, \(T_C=60+2.961(4+1)=74.81\;^\circ\mathrm C\), only 1.19 °C below the 76 °C low trip threshold. The installed thermal path is not verified.

The auxiliary supply screen uses the 50 VA Triad VPT30-1670, 4,700 µF per raw rail and revised regulators. All 216 rectifier cases pass the raw-voltage screen; maximum half-winding RMS is 0.725 A against 1.67 A, with 8.436 W maximum total regulator heat. The original ±15 V regulator input windows fail at low line, so this transformer result applies only with the revised regulator configuration. The flat-base Fischer SK 81 50 SA guard envelope is 24.88 mm deep: it has 5.12 mm clearance in the 130 mm tray depth screen and is 9.88 mm too deep for the 145 mm cage. The Boyd 530002B02500G guarded rear-wall envelope is rejected. Actual wall position, rear-cover and withdrawal clearance, installed thermal resistance, wiring, acoustic performance and all hardware remain unverified; do not place or build from these models.

FB-176/178 drill measurements

FB-176 reported 21 cm from the drill-bit tip to the tool back and 28 mm from the bit to the chuck. The first value converts to \(21\;\mathrm{cm}\times10=210\;\mathrm{mm}\). FB-176 also reported a 39 mm widest chuck. FB-178 clarified that the widest chuck diameter is 46.5 mm and the motor housing is 51 mm wide. This resolves the earlier chuck conflict; the 39 mm report remains part of the measurement history.

With the proposed 2 mm clearance allowance on each side, the chuck cross-section is \(46.5+2(2)=50.5\;\mathrm{mm}\). The corresponding axis-to-obstruction distance is \(46.5/2+2=25.25\;\mathrm{mm}\), only where the obstruction overlaps the chuck axially. For the motor housing, the same allowance gives \(51+2(2)=55\;\mathrm{mm}\) across its width. Do not treat the housing as a circular envelope centered on the drill axis.

The exact tool model, measurement setup, and uncertainty are not verified. Housing height and axis offset, chuck axial length, and fastener positions remain unknown. Full approach, withdrawal, stabilization, and screw access remain unverified. These dimensions do not establish swept clearance or a pass.

Vocabulary

Compensation
Parts used to control feedback response and reduce the risk of oscillation.
IDSS
Drain current at zero gate-to-source voltage under stated test conditions.
Threshold voltage
A model or data-sheet value used to describe when transistor conduction changes.
Square-law model
A simplified transistor model in which current depends on the square of a voltage difference.
Current-balance error
The difference between currents entering and leaving a connection point.
Safe operating area
Allowed combinations of voltage, current, temperature and time for a device.
AC
Alternating current; a small-signal frequency test in this record.
Compensation
A capacitor or network used to control feedback-loop response.
DC operating point
The steady voltage and current values before a signal is applied.
Generic MOSFET model
An authored transistor approximation. It is not a manufacturer model or selected device.
Ideal sink
A simulated current draw that holds its value without real-device limits.
Phase margin
A feedback-stability measure at the frequency where loop gain reaches unity.
Servo sink
A feedback circuit that adjusts transistor current using a sensed voltage.
THD
Total harmonic distortion; harmonic signal content relative to the fundamental.
Transient run
A time-domain simulation of changing voltages and currents.
Valid model domain
The voltage and current range where the model represents supported behavior.
Internal node guess
A starting estimate for a simulator's numerical solution. It does not set or force the final voltage.
.nodeset
An ngspice command that gives initial voltage guesses while allowing the solver to find its own final operating point.
Load-only current floor
Minimum peak output current calculated for the stated load alone. It excludes feedback, gate charging, tolerances, and faults.
Standing loss
Power dissipated at the stated DC bias before the output signal is applied.
Sink-to-air thermal resistance
Temperature rise of the shared heat sink divided by its total heat flow to air, in °C/W.
Motor-body offset
Distance between the drill axis and the outer motor housing.
Case-to-air thermal resistance
Temperature rise from a device case to ambient air per watt through the case cooling path, in K/W.
Slew rate
Maximum rate of output-voltage change, expressed in V/µs.
NC
Not connected; the named package pin has no intentional circuit connection.
Floating pin
A pin left without an intentional connection.
Admittance
Current divided by voltage for an AC load, expressed here in siemens.
Impedance
Voltage divided by current for an AC load, expressed here in ohms.
External-only peak current
Peak current through the modeled external load, excluding current in internal feedback paths.
