CHINA 220 V POWER-SUPPLY DESIGN: MUST-REMEMBERS
============================================

SAFETY WARNING
Mains voltage and tube-amplifier power supplies can be lethal, including after
unplugging. Have the mains wiring, protective earthing, and insulation reviewed
by a qualified professional before energizing. [1]


1. CHINESE MAINS REQUIREMENTS
----------------------------

1. Design for 220 V AC RMS, 50 Hz.
   China's normal single-phase supply is 220 V; 380 V is the three-phase
   line-to-line voltage—not an alternative connection for a 220 V appliance. [2]

2. Do not design around exactly 220 V.
   The normal single-phase supply tolerance is +7% / -10%, giving 198–235.4 V.
   Specify the equipment's operating range separately, and assess surges and
   abnormal conditions beyond normal variation.

3. Require an explicit 50 Hz transformer rating.
   A 60-Hz-only transformer can saturate and overheat at 50 Hz, even when its
   voltage rating looks correct. “220 V” alone is insufficient. [3]

4. Remember RMS versus peak voltage.
   For a sinusoidal supply:

       V_peak = sqrt(2) × V_RMS

   Therefore, 220 V RMS is approximately 311 V peak. A bridge rectifier and
   reservoir capacitor do not turn mains into a safe, isolated supply. [4]


2. ISOLATION, EARTHING, AND MAINS CONNECTIONS
-------------------------------------------

5. Provide safety isolation for user-accessible circuits.
   A low output voltage does not by itself establish safety. An autotransformer
   has an electrical connection between input and output; it is not a substitute
   for an isolating transformer. [5]

6. Use protective earth for the metal-chassis tube-amp build.
   Provide a dedicated, mechanically secure chassis-earth connection with
   conductive contact and locking hardware—not a connection dependent on paint,
   an audio jack, or incidental mounting contact. [6]

7. Never switch, fuse, or insert a resistor into protective earth.
   Never disconnect earth to solve hum; address the signal-ground arrangement
   instead. [1]

8. Keep neutral, protective earth, and signal return conceptually separate.
   Do not use neutral as chassis earth or add an internal neutral-to-earth bond
   in the appliance. [6]

9. Protect the live conductor; never fuse only neutral.
   For this build, use a suitably rated double-pole mains switch to disconnect
   both live and neutral. The protective-earth connection remains
   uninterrupted. [7]

10. Specify the complete power-entry assembly.
    Use a Chinese-standard cord set, appropriate inlet, strain relief, insulated
    terminals, and hardware rated for the operating voltage, current, and
    temperature. China's current plug-and-socket dimensional standard is
    GB 1002-2024. [8]


3. FUSES, SWITCHING, AND SURGE PROTECTION
---------------------------------------

11. Select the fuse from actual input requirements.
    Consider RMS current, startup pulses, ambient temperature, time-current
    characteristics, and breaking capacity. Simply halving the old 120 V fuse
    rating is not a complete selection method. [9]

12. Check voltage ratings as well as amperage.
    A 125 V fuse is unsuitable for 220 V mains. Likewise, an AC rating does not
    establish suitability for interrupting a high-voltage DC rail. Verify the
    exact fuse and holder specifications. [9]

13. Design for inrush current.
    Transformer energization and capacitor charging must not damage switches,
    rectifiers, or wiring. Check both cold startup and rapid off–on cycling;
    a hot NTC thermistor has less resistance and provides less limiting. [6]

14. Treat MOV protection as a coordinated safety circuit.
    Select the MOV's continuous-voltage and surge ratings, coordinate its
    protection, and address thermal runaway. Do not assume an ordinary upstream
    fuse will always protect an overheating MOV. [10]

15. Use the correct safety capacitors.
    Across live–neutral, use the appropriate X-rated component; from mains to
    protective earth, use the appropriate Y-rated component. An ordinary
    capacitor with a high DC voltage marking is not an equivalent substitute. [11]

16. Budget total leakage current.
    Y capacitors and multiple mains filters contribute to earth leakage; assess
    the complete assembly rather than each module independently. [6]


4. TRANSFORMER SPECIFICATION AND WINDING CONNECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------

17. Obtain loaded and unloaded secondary voltages.
    Also require winding-current ratings, total VA, regulation, insulation
    details, temperature limits, and thermal-protection information—not just
    a marketplace description. [12]

18. Do not equate secondary AC current with available DC current.
    Capacitor-input rectifiers draw charging pulses; transformer heating depends
    on winding RMS current. [12]

19. Follow the manufacturer's winding-phasing diagram.
    Dual primaries connected in series must have the correct phasing. Do not
    infer connections from wire colors alone. [13]

20. Do not casually parallel secondary windings.
    Confirm that parallel operation is permitted, with matching voltages and
    correct phasing. Two separate 6.3 V windings are not automatically
    interchangeable with one larger winding. [13]


5. RECTIFICATION, FILTERING, AND STORED ENERGY
--------------------------------------------

21. Calculate full-wave reservoir ripple at 100 Hz—not 120 Hz.
    The approximate capacitor-input relationship is:

        DeltaV_pp ≈ I_load / (2 × f_mains × C)

    Consequently, changing from 60 Hz to 50 Hz gives approximately 20% more
    ripple at the same load and capacitance, under this approximation.

22. Rate capacitors for maximum voltage, not normal operating voltage.
    Include maximum mains voltage, transformer regulation, startup, and
    light-load conditions. [12]

23. Choose rectifiers for the actual topology.
    Check repetitive reverse voltage, charging-current peaks, startup surge,
    and heat—not merely average output current. [12]

24. Check capacitor ripple-current ratings and temperature.
    Adequate capacitance and voltage ratings do not guarantee acceptable
    heating or lifetime. [12]

25. Provide permanent discharge paths for hazardous capacitor banks.
    A switch or connector must not unintentionally separate the capacitor from
    its bleeder. Calculate discharge time and resistor dissipation, including:

        P = V^2 / R

    Check the resistor's voltage rating as well as its wattage. [14]

26. Never assume “off” means discharged.
    An extinguished indicator or an elapsed waiting period is not proof.
    Verify capacitor voltage with correctly rated equipment before contact. [1]


6. PCB, ENCLOSURE, AND THERMAL DESIGN
-----------------------------------

27. Calculate creepage and clearance rather than choosing a generic gap.
    Requirements depend on working voltage, transient voltage, insulation
    category, material, pollution degree, and altitude—not just “220 V.” [15]

28. Check the complete physical insulation barrier.
    Include PCB copper, component leads, connectors, mounting screws, heatsinks,
    and enclosure surfaces. A compliant component can become unsafe through
    incorrect mounting. [6]

29. Account for humidity, contamination, and altitude.
    Condensation and contamination affect surface insulation; operation above
    2,000 m can require increased clearance. Do not assume one layout is suitable
    for every location in China. [15]

30. Validate temperatures inside the finished enclosure.
    Check the transformer, rectifier, regulators, power resistors, wiring, and
    capacitors under sustained load. An open-bench temperature test does not
    establish enclosed operating temperatures. [16]


7. SPECIFIC REMINDERS FOR YOUR TUBE AMPLIFIER
-------------------------------------------

31. Convert every mains-powered branch—not only the main transformer.
    Your existing relay/control supply also needs a 220 V/50 Hz-compatible input
    arrangement. Internal heater, relay, and logic rails retain their required
    operating voltages; they do not all become 220 V.

32. Include the added reverb tubes in the heater budget.
    One 12AT7 plus one 12AX7 adds 0.6 A at 6.3 V for the cited JJ equivalents.
    Budget their high-voltage circuit current separately. [17]

33. Do not confuse center-tapped and bridge rectification.
    A 290–0–290 V winding is 580 V end-to-end. A capacitor-input bridge across
    both ends can approach 820 V, before losses—not approximately 410 V. [4]

34. Check heater-to-cathode voltage limits.
    They differ between tube types and manufacturers. A heater winding elevated
    above chassis potential is not automatically a suitable touch-safe supply
    for external controls. [17]

35. Keep footswitch connections safely low-voltage and isolated from hazardous
    rails.
    Check the complete separation path, including transformer windings, relay
    contact-to-coil insulation, PCB spacing, and connector wiring. [15]


8. VERIFICATION AND CHINA SOURCING
--------------------------------

36. Require documentation for safety-critical parts.
    Obtain exact manufacturer part numbers, winding diagrams, ratings, and
    relevant approval records. A seller's “220 V compatible” claim is not enough
    to establish 50 Hz operation or adequate insulation. [3]

37. Include electrical-safety testing in the design plan.
    Have qualified personnel assess protective-earth bonding, insulation
    strength, leakage/touch current, thermal behavior, and applicable
    abnormal-fault conditions. [6]

38. Do not mistake a Variac for isolation.
    A conventional variable autotransformer adjusts voltage but retains an
    electrical connection to mains. [18]

39. Never lift an oscilloscope's protective earth.
    Use appropriately rated differential or isolated measurement equipment for
    floating measurements; a conventional scope ground clip is
    earth-referenced. [19]

40. Use the applicable Chinese safety standard, not an obsolete schematic's
    assumptions.
    GB 4943.1-2022 is the current audio/video/ICT safety standard and replaced
    GB 8898-2011. For a product intended for sale, separately establish the
    applicable certification and EMC requirements. [20]
