POWER-CIRCUIT DESIGN MUST-REMEMBERS
=================================

Design imperatives for a 220 V / 50 Hz tube-amplifier build.


MAINS AND SAFETY
---------------

1. Design for the input range—not just nominal voltage.
   Specify minimum and maximum mains voltage. Both the main transformer
   and auxiliary supply must support your 220 V / 50 Hz requirement; do
   not assume a 60 Hz-only transformer is suitable.
   Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf

2. Provide proper mains isolation.
   Keep the audio circuit and accessible controls isolated from mains.
   An isolated transformer's high-voltage secondary can still deliver a
   dangerous shock.
   Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf

3. Protective earth is permanent.
   Bond the metal chassis using a dedicated, mechanically secure earth
   connection. Never switch, fuse, or disconnect protective earth to
   cure hum.
   Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/Grounding.pdf

4. A fuse needs more than the correct amp rating.
   Check voltage rating, AC/DC suitability, breaking capacity,
   temperature, and time–current behavior. It must tolerate normal
   startup without permitting damaging fault currents.
   Source: https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/fuseology-selection-guide?assetguid=e0a12d55-c89a-4e06-94e0-9fe3281f889f

5. Use safety-rated capacitors in mains-filter positions.
   Use the appropriate X classification across the mains and Y
   classification for line-to-earth filtering—not an ordinary capacitor
   with a seemingly adequate voltage rating.
   Source: https://www.vishay.com/docs/48251/_checklist_safety_caps_vmn_ms7357_1707.pdf

6. Determine creepage and clearance properly.
   Required spacing depends on working voltage, transients, insulation
   category, materials, pollution, and altitude. There is no single
   spacing that makes every high-voltage circuit safe.
   Source: https://www.ti.com/video/6146024211001


LOADS AND COMPONENT RATINGS
--------------------------

7. Budget every load before choosing the transformer.
   Include power tubes, screens, preamp stages, added reverb tubes,
   heaters, relays, indicators, and bleeders—not just the advertised
   amplifier output power.
   Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/smoothing.html

8. Transformer winding current is not the same as delivered DC current.
   Rectifier topology and capacitor-charging pulses determine the
   required winding RMS-current rating.
   Source: https://www.hammfg.com/electronics/transformers/rectifier

9. Calculate maximum voltage at high mains, startup, and no load.
   Before tubes conduct, the supply can rise substantially. Check EVERY
   filter capacitor, not only the first reservoir capacitor.
   Source: https://www.hammfg.com/electronics/transformers/rectifier

10. Rate rectifiers for reverse voltage and surge current.
    Average current alone is insufficient. Bridge and center-tapped
    arrangements impose different stresses; calculate for the actual
    circuit.
    Source: https://www.hammfg.com/electronics/transformers/rectifier

11. Resistors have voltage and pulse limits as well as wattage limits.
    Check continuous dissipation, startup pulses, and maximum working
    voltage—especially for dropping, balancing, and discharge resistors.
    Source: https://origin-www.vishay.com/en/resistors-fixed/

12. Design thermally, not just electrically.
    Use the temperature inside the enclosure, not room temperature.
    Allow cooling space and keep electrolytic capacitors away from hot
    tubes and power resistors.
    Source: https://www.tdk-electronics.tdk.com/download/185386/31a5416e653dd6e4e428b8208d65cc2e/pdf-generaltechnicalinformation.pdf


CAPACITORS, FILTERING, AND STORED ENERGY
--------------------------------------

13. Bigger reservoir capacitors are not automatically better.
    More capacitance reduces ripple but increases charging-current
    stress. With a tube rectifier, respect its permitted reservoir
    capacitance and required source impedance.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/smoothing.html

14. Check capacitor ripple-current rating—not merely capacitance and voltage.
    Ripple current causes internal heating. ESR, frequency, ambient
    temperature, and lifetime all matter.
    Source: https://www.tdk-electronics.tdk.com/download/185386/31a5416e653dd6e4e428b8208d65cc2e/pdf-generaltechnicalinformation.pdf

15. Series capacitors do not automatically share voltage equally.
    Account for leakage differences and provide suitable balancing.
    Two identical capacitors in series give half the capacitance of one.
    Source: https://www.tdk-electronics.tdk.com/download/185386/31a5416e653dd6e4e428b8208d65cc2e/pdf-generaltechnicalinformation.pdf

16. Provide discharge paths for all high-voltage capacitor banks.
    Ensure switches cannot leave a charged section isolated from its
    bleeder. Verify residual voltage before touching anything:
    UNPLUGGED DOES NOT MEAN DISCHARGED.
    Source: https://fluke.com.au/en-au/learn/blog/digital-multimeters/how-to-measure-capacitance


GROUNDING AND PHYSICAL LAYOUT
----------------------------

17. Design the return-current paths explicitly.
    Ground symbols do not make physical wires electrically identical.
    Keep rectifier-charging and power-stage currents out of sensitive
    input and reverb-recovery returns.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/Grounding.pdf

18. Keep the transformer–rectifier–reservoir charging loop compact.
    Connect that noisy section directly together rather than letting its
    current circulate through the audio ground wiring.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/Grounding.pdf

19. Provide local filtering and decoupling.
    A large capacitor elsewhere in the enclosure is not a substitute
    for a short local supply-and-return loop. Give sensitive amplifier
    stages appropriately filtered supply nodes.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/smoothing.html

20. Wiring and connectors are part of the safety design.
    Check current capacity, insulation voltage, temperature suitability,
    mechanical security, and strain relief—not merely whether the
    connector fits.
    Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf


TUBE HEATERS AND SWITCHING CONTROLS
---------------------------------

21. Check heater voltage under actual load.
    Include every added tube in the current budget, provide a deliberate
    heater reference, and check heater-to-cathode voltage limits—including
    during startup.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/heater.html

22. Do not casually share an elevated heater supply with grounded controls.
    Connecting a ground-referenced relay circuit can defeat the heater
    elevation. Establish the reference and isolation arrangement before
    sharing a winding.
    Source: https://www.valvewizard.co.uk/heater.html

23. Protect relay drivers from inductive kickback.
    Use an appropriately rated driver and flyback/clamp arrangement.
    Account for the current when multiple relays are energized together.
    Source: https://www.ti.com/product/ULN2003V12

24. Keep the footswitch interface safely low voltage.
    Maintain adequate insulation between its accessible wiring and
    mains/high-voltage circuits—including across relay contacts and
    coils. A low nominal supply voltage alone does not establish safety.
    Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf

25. Check regulators at both extremes.
    Minimum input must provide enough regulation headroom; maximum input
    and load must not overheat the device. Follow its output-capacitor
    and layout requirements.
    Source: https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/tps715


FAILURE MODES AND VERIFICATION
------------------------------

26. Check more than normal operation.
    Review startup, shutdown, no load, overload, and credible
    component-open/component-short failures. Protection must work when
    something fails—not only when everything is healthy.
    Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf

27. Treat oscilloscope grounding as part of the circuit.
    An ordinary bench scope's ground clip is usually earth-connected.
    Use correctly rated differential or isolated measurement equipment
    where needed; never lift the scope's protective earth.
    Source: https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/oscilloscopes/abcs-of-portable-oscilloscopes-part-3-triggering-and-isolation

28. Require physical verification before calling the design finished.
    Check protective-earth integrity, insulation, loaded voltages,
    ripple, temperature, and discharge behavior through a planned
    commissioning process with qualified high-voltage supervision.
    A correct-looking schematic is not proof of a safe build.
    Source: https://e2e.ti.com/cfs-file/__key/telligent-evolution-components-attachments/00-196-01-00-00-99-38-35/UL_5F00_Power_5F00_Supply.pdf
